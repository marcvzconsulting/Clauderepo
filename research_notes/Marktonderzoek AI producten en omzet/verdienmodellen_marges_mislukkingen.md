# Business economics of AI products: margins, dependence, retention, pricing, failures, ARR inflation, profitability (state: November 2026)

Method note for the report writer: all findings below come from web-search result summaries gathered on 2026-11-25. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (techcrunch.com and chartmogul.com did not resolve), so no figure was checked against the full text of its primary source. Each finding carries a label: **[company]** = stated by the company itself, **[reported]** = press report based on sources/leaks, **[estimate]** = third-party estimate or aggregator, **[survey]** = benchmark survey, **[opinion]** = commentary. Almost nothing here is audited; private AI companies do not publish financial statements. Where sources conflict, both are shown.

## 1. What gross margins do AI application companies report versus classic SaaS (70-80%), and how have falling token prices and heavier agentic usage changed this in 2026?

### Takeaway
AI application gross margins sit well below classic SaaS: survey averages are around 45% (2025) to 52-53% (2026), and the fastest-growing coding/agent products have reported margins ranging from negative to roughly 35%. Token prices fell sharply in 2026, but agentic workflows consume so many more tokens per task that total inference bills kept rising; margin improvement has come mainly from own/cheaper models, model routing, enterprise mix and moving cost to the customer via credits.

### Cited Findings
- **[survey]** ICONIQ's January 2026 State of AI snapshot puts average AI product gross margin around 52% for 2026, up from 41% in 2024 (some sources cite 45% for 2025). — [SaaStr on ICONIQ snapshot](https://www.saastr.com/iconiqs-latest-state-of-ai-report-the-10-most-important-data-points-for-saas-founders); primary PDF: [ICONIQ, "The Execution Era of AI", Jan 2026](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/65d0d38fc4ec8ce8a8921654/6979532decf89bd3df2163b0_ICONIQ_Analytics_Insights_2026_State_of_AI_Bi-Annual_Snapshot.pdf)
- **[survey]** ICONIQ's later 2026 report ("The Builder's Economy", survey described as Q2 2026) reportedly projects 53% for 2026 and 59% for 2027. — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-builders-economy-10-metrics-from-iconiqs-newest-2026-state-of-ai-report/); dating per [Offset Partners](https://www.offsetpartners.io/resources/ai-startup-gross-margin-benchmarks)
- **[survey]** ICONIQ: model inference rises from 20% of total AI product cost at pre-launch to 23% at scaling stage, while talent falls from 32% to 26%. Several blogs misquote the 23% as a share of revenue; Offset Partners says it is a share of total cost. — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/iconiqs-latest-state-of-ai-report-the-10-most-important-data-points-for-saas-founders); correction in [Offset Partners](https://www.offsetpartners.io/resources/ai-startup-gross-margin-benchmarks)
- **[survey, dated Aug 2025]** Bessemer State of AI 2025: "Supernovas" (fastest growers, thin-wrapper products) averaged roughly 25% gross margin, often negative, with about $1.13M ARR per FTE; "Shooting Stars" averaged about 60% gross margin and reached about $103M ARR in year four. No 2026 Bessemer edition was found. — [Decasonic](https://www.decasonic.com/perspectives/from-narrative-premium-to-proof-premium); [1752vc](https://www.1752.vc/learn/ai-gross-margins-and-inference-costs)
- **[reported/estimate]** Cursor: gross margin of -23% for the quarter ended January 2026 (figures attributed to Contrary Research); an earlier estimate had Cursor paying Anthropic about $650M a year against about $500M annualised revenue. — [Tech Economics Substack, "The $60 billion company with negative gross margins"](https://techeconomics.substack.com/p/the-60-billion-company-with-negative)
- **[reported]** Cursor: TechCrunch reported in April 2026 (17 April, four sources per one summary) that Cursor had reached slight gross-margin profitability: positive on large enterprise accounts, still negative on individual developers, helped by the in-house Composer model and routing to cheaper models. — secondary summaries: [Tech Economics Substack](https://techeconomics.substack.com/p/the-60-billion-company-with-negative); [ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/how-does-cursor-make-money-subscriptions-token-pricing-and-the-business-model-breakdown)
- **[estimate]** Cursor at roughly $4B annualised revenue by May 2026, from $100M in January 2025 (aggregator figures). — [Sacra, Cursor](https://sacra.com/c/cursor/); [DevGraphiq](https://devgraphiq.com/cursor-statistics/)
- **[reported]** Cursor's Composer 2.5 lists at $0.50/$2.50 per million input/output tokens (standard) and $3.00/$15.00 (Fast); it is post-trained from Moonshot AI's open-source Kimi K2.5 rather than trained from scratch; paid plans split usage into an "Auto + Composer" pool on Cursor's own model and a more expensive API pool for third-party frontier models. — [Capital and Compute](https://capitalandcompute.net/blog/cursor-composer-2-5-real-cost/); [Coworker.ai](https://coworker.ai/blog/cursor-pricing)
- **[reported]** Replit: The Information reported gross margins swinging between roughly -14% and +36% during 2025; the CEO claims enterprise margins of 80-90%. One aggregator gives ~85% attributed to Sacra, unconfirmed. — [Wing VC](https://www.wing.vc/content/who-makes-money-when-inference-gets-10x-cheaper); [Clinkbill](https://clinkbill.com/arr-leaderboard/replit)
- **[reported, unverified]** Lovable: a leaked pitch deck reported by Sifted put gross margin at 36% with a target of 65% by end 2026; another source estimates about 35% for 2025; a third states Lovable, Bolt, Replit and v0 have never disclosed gross margin and warns that credit pricing can hide worsening unit economics. — [ValueAdd VC, Lovable valuation](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/lovable-valuation-2026-13-2b-and-500m-arr-how-vibe-coding-actually-makes-money); [ValueAdd VC, deep dive](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/lovable-vibe-coding-app-builder-company-deep-dive)
- **[company]** Wix said Base44's gross margin was "nearly zero" at the start of 2026 and targeted about 60% after moving to its own model. — [SaaStr, "Inference is the New Sales & Marketing Spend"](https://www.saastr.com/inference-is-the-new-sales-marketing-spend/)
- **[company]** Perplexity has never disclosed gross margin, inference costs or profit; it signed a three-year, $750M Microsoft Azure contract in January 2026. Annualised revenue: over $450M in March 2026 (FT), above $750M in August 2026 (The Information, third-party estimate); CEO told CNBC in June 2026 revenue had tripled since the start of the year. — [ProvenStartups](https://provenstartups.com/blog/revenue-reality/perplexity-revenue); [Penchan](https://penchan.co/en/market/ai/perplexity/perplexity-valuation/)
- **[estimate]** Token prices: an analysis of 2.4 billion enterprise API calls found blended cost fell 67% year on year, from $18.40 to $6.07 per million tokens (Q1 2025 to Q1 2026); a Silicon Data index showed $2.04 (end May 2026) falling to $0.97 (1 Sept 2026); Epoch AI: at fixed capability, cost per token falls roughly 5-10x a year. — [AI Business Weekly](https://aibusinessweekly.net/p/ai-token-prices-record-lows-inference-costs); [Navya AI cost report](https://www.navyaai.com/reports/ai-cost-report-token-prices-vs-ai-bill)
- **[estimate]** Usage outpaces price cuts: Gartner (March 2026) says agentic tasks consume 5-30x more tokens per task than a standard chatbot; Ramp billing data reportedly shows token usage up 1,001% from January 2025 to April 2026 while dollar spend grew 497%; EY reportedly found a single agentic customer-service interaction rose from about $0.04 (2023) to $1.20 (2026); Gartner (August 2026) projects inference cost per agentic workflow to rise more than fivefold through 2028. These figures are repeated across vendor blogs and were not traced to the primary reports. — [HackerNoon, "The Token Price Fallacy"](https://hackernoon.com/the-token-price-fallacy-why-your-agentic-ai-bill-keeps-growing-while-unit-costs-collapse); [Sista AI](https://sista.ai/en/insights/ai-agent-token-costs-rising); [The Source Code](https://www.the-sourcecode.com/ai-tech/token-costs-enterprise-ai-business-case-2026)
- **[company]** GitHub Copilot: GitHub called its June 2026 move to token-metered billing "an important step toward a sustainable, reliable Copilot business" (see section 4). No source found gave Copilot's gross margin or a per-user loss figure. — [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/)

### Inferences
- The gap versus SaaS is structural: every unit of usage carries a variable cost paid to a model provider, so revenue growth does not automatically widen margin. The reported pattern (negative on individuals, positive on enterprise at Cursor; -14% to +36% at Replit) suggests margin depends more on customer mix and price plan than on token prices.
- Cheaper tokens did not rescue margins by themselves in 2026, because agent products spend the savings on more tokens per task. The margin levers that worked were supply-side substitution (own model, open-weight base, routing) and demand-side metering (credits).
- Survey averages of 52-53% and case figures of -23% to 36% describe different populations: surveys cover mostly B2B products with AI features; the cases are the highest-usage coding/agent tools. A reader should not apply the survey average to a high-growth wrapper.

### Gaps
- No gross-margin figure was found for Notion AI, Intercom Fin, GitHub Copilot or Perplexity; companies do not disclose them.
- The April 2026 TechCrunch Cursor article and the Sifted Lovable deck could not be opened; figures are from secondary summaries.
- No audited figure exists for any private company named here.

## 2. How dependent are AI apps on model providers, and what happened when providers changed prices/rate limits or launched competing features?

### Takeaway
Dependence is high and one-directional: the provider sets cost, access and capacity, and also competes with its customers. Documented episodes (Anthropic cutting Windsurf off with under five days' notice, Cursor's 2025 repricing and apology, Jasper's stall after ChatGPT) show that an application without its own model or distribution can lose margin or demand through decisions it does not control.

### Cited Findings
- **[reported, June 2025]** Anthropic cut nearly all of Windsurf's first-party access to Claude 3.x models with less than five days' notice, per Windsurf CEO Varun Mohan, shortly after reports that OpenAI would acquire Windsurf. Anthropic co-founder Jared Kaplan: "It would be odd for us to be selling Claude to OpenAI", and Anthropic wanted to prioritise capacity for customers it would work with sustainably. Windsurf also had no direct Claude 4 access at launch while Cursor, Devin and GitHub Copilot did; Windsurf's workaround was bring-your-own API key. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-cuts-claude-access-for-windsurf-after-openais-3b-takeover-news/); [Yahoo Finance/TechCrunch](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/anthropic-co-founder-cutting-access-193549089.html); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johanmoreno/2025/06/05/anthropic-cuts-windsurfs-claude-access-before-openai-acquisition/)
- **[reported]** Windsurf ended as a "reverse acqui-hire": Bloomberg (via Foundra) counts at least six deals since March 2024 where a tech giant hired founders/researchers and licensed the technology, leaving the company behind, including Google with Windsurf and Character.AI, Microsoft with Inflection, Amazon with Adept and Covariant. — [Foundra](https://www.foundra.ai/key-reads/startup-shutdown-data-2026-premortem-first-time-founders)
- **[company, June-July 2025]** Cursor moved its $20 Pro plan from a request count to usage-based credits in June 2025; after user backlash it apologised on 4 July 2025 ("We missed the mark ... we didn't handle this pricing rollout well and we're sorry") and offered refunds for unexpected charges between 16 June and 4 July. After the fix, Pro meant unlimited Tab and Auto plus $20 of frontier-model usage at API prices; the usage limits stayed. — [Cursor Community on X](https://x.com/cursorcommunity/status/1941510465281986792); [FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/magazine/articles/cursor-pricing-change-user-backlash-refund); [Vantage](https://www.vantage.sh/blog/cursor-pricing-explained)
- **[opinion — Ed Zitron]** Zitron argues Cursor is Anthropic's largest customer, deeply unprofitable, and directly competed against by Anthropic's Claude Code; he calls Cursor the weak point of the AI bubble and frames provider price rises as a "subprime AI crisis" passed down to customers. He acknowledges Anthropic does not break out revenue by customer; his figures are source-based estimates. — [Where's Your Ed At, "Anthropic and OpenAI Have Begun The Subprime AI Crisis"](https://www.wheresyoured.at/anthropic-and-openai-have-begun-the-subprime-ai-crisis/); counter-view (cost control is feasible) in [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/how-solid-is-ed-zitrons-case-against-generative-ai/?p=22802486)
- **[reported]** Cursor's response to dependence in 2026: an in-house model (Composer, built on an open-weight base) and a separate, cheaper usage pool for its own models; see section 1. — [Capital and Compute](https://capitalandcompute.net/blog/cursor-composer-2-5-real-cost/)
- **[reported, 2023]** Jasper: recurring revenue doubled in 2022 to $80M; after ChatGPT created "a formidable low-cost competitor practically overnight", Jasper cut its 2023 ARR forecast by at least 30%, laid off staff in July 2023 (less than nine months after a $125M raise), cut its internal valuation by 20% (to roughly $1.2B from $1.5B), and the co-founders stepped back in September 2023. — [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/jasper-cuts-internal-valuation-as-ai-growth-slows/); [Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2023/07/17/jasper-ai-laying-off-staff-9-months-after-125m-raise/); original: [The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/jasper-an-early-generative-ai-winner-cuts-internal-valuation-as-growth-slows?rc=frme3w)
- **[estimate, unverified]** One review claims Jasper has been flat at roughly $80M ARR for three years and had a second layoff round in 2024; single unverified source. — [OpenAI Tools Hub review](https://www.openaitoolshub.org/ai-product-research/jasper-ai)
- **[reported, 2026]** The labs also kill their own products when economics fail: OpenAI shut down the Sora app in 2026 (high operating cost, poor retention) and discontinued the ChatGPT Atlas browser in August 2026, folding its features into ChatGPT. — [TechCrunch AI graveyard](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/the-ai-graveyard-a-running-list-of-projects-and-startups-that-didnt-make-it/)

### Inferences
- Three distinct dependence risks appear in the record: access risk (Windsurf), cost pass-through risk (Cursor 2025), and substitution risk (Jasper versus ChatGPT; Cursor versus Claude Code). They call for different defences: multi-provider routing, own models, and workflow/data lock-in respectively.
- Enterprise contracts and own models are how the larger apps reduced exposure in 2026; small wrappers have neither.

### Gaps
- No search result covered Claude Code weekly rate limits, Replit's effort-based pricing backlash, or any 2026 provider price increase in detail; these were asked but not found.
- No reliable figure for the share of an app's revenue paid to model providers beyond the Cursor estimate.

## 3. What do retention and churn data show for AI products?

### Takeaway
AI-native products retain roughly half as well as classic B2B SaaS at the median (about 40% gross revenue retention versus 82-88%), and the gap is almost entirely a price-point/segment effect: tools above $250 a month retain like normal SaaS, tools under $50 a month churn most of their revenue within a year. Consumer AI apps convert and monetise better up front but lose subscribers faster.

### Cited Findings
- **[survey, data to Sept 2025]** ChartMogul, "The SaaS Retention Report: The AI churn wave", about 3,500 software companies: AI-native median gross revenue retention (GRR) 40% and net revenue retention (NRR) 48%, versus 82% GRR for B2B SaaS (a Forbes piece cites 88%); AI-native NRR is below B2C SaaS (49%). AI-native median GRR rose from 27% in January 2025 to 40% in September 2025. — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/04/08/seed-stage-ai-startups-are-flashing-record-revenue-numbers-and-most-of-them-are-not-what-they-seem/)
- **[survey]** ChartMogul by price: under $50/month, 23% GRR and 32% NRR; above $250/month, 70% GRR and 85% NRR, close to traditional B2B SaaS. ChartMogul calls the AI-native results directional because some segments contain only about 50 companies. — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/)
- **[survey, March 2026]** RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 (over 115,000 apps, more than $16B revenue): AI apps retain worse on monthly and annual plans; 12-month retention on monthly plans 6.1% (AI) versus 9.5% (non-AI); annual-plan retention 21.1% versus 30.7%. — [RevenueCat report](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)
- **[survey]** RevenueCat: AI apps monetise better up front: trial-to-paid conversion 8.5% versus 5.6% at the median; year-1 realised LTV per payer $30.16 versus $21.37 (about 41% more); monthly revenue per payer $18.92 versus $13.59. Weekly-plan retention figures conflict between summaries (TechCrunch: 2.5% vs 1.7% in favour of AI; PPC Land: 1.2% vs 1.7%). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)
- **[survey]** RevenueCat study of 3,519 AI apps: the high-retention group keeps a median 13.9% of paid subscriptions active after a year versus 1.4% for the low-retention group. — [RevenueCat AI app retention study](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ai-app-retention-study)
- **[opinion/analysis]** a16z coined "AI tourists": users who try a product out of curiosity and churn hard between weeks 2 and 5; a16z suggests measuring retention from month three rather than month zero to exclude them. — [Userpilot summary](https://userpilot.com/blog/retention-curve/); [Array VC, Feb 2026](https://www.array.vc/insights/churn-retention-in-the-era-of-ai-feb-2026)
- **[company claim, secondhand]** Lovable reportedly claims NRR above 100% and 85% day-30 retention of paying customers; one source attributes the NRR to usage top-ups. — [Quasa teardown](https://quasa.io/media/lovable-0-400m-arr-in-14-months-a-brutal-teardown-of-the-playbook-and-what-s-probably-wrong-with-it)
- **[opinion]** Greg Isenberg: "curiosity revenue" from novelty buyers; poor retention after 3-6 months is the warning sign. — [Greg Isenberg, "Vibe Revenue"](https://www.gregisenberg.com/blog/vibe-revenue)

### Inferences
- Measuring from month three, as a16z proposes, makes retention curves look better without changing cash collected; a reader comparing companies must check which base is used.
- High up-front conversion plus fast churn means AI consumer apps depend on continuous paid acquisition; with inference as a variable cost on top, a monthly cohort can be unprofitable even at high revenue.
- NRR above 100% driven by credit top-ups is usage expansion, which also expands inference cost; it is weaker evidence of durable value than seat expansion in SaaS.

### Gaps
- No 2026 edition of the ChartMogul benchmark was confirmed; the latest verified data runs to September 2025. One 2026 article describes an August 2026 report with the same ~40% figure, but its existence could not be confirmed.
- No ICONIQ or Bessemer retention benchmark for AI products was found in this session.
- No churn data for Lovable, Replit, Cursor or Perplexity beyond company claims.

## 4. Which pricing models are emerging and how do they affect margin and predictability?

### Takeaway
Pure seat pricing is shrinking; the 2026 norm is a hybrid of a base seat or platform fee plus credits or usage, with outcome pricing visible but rare. Credits protect vendor margin by passing token cost to the customer, at the price of less predictable bills and visible customer resistance.

### Cited Findings
- **[survey]** Growth Unhinged (Kyle Poyar) survey of 230 B2B software and AI companies, April-May 2026: hybrid models at 37% (up from 25%); 29% offer AI credits. Cited via a secondary page. — [Lowob research summary](https://lowob.com/research/seats-usage-or-outcomes/)
- **[survey]** ICONIQ State of AI (secondary coverage): consumption pricing rose from 35% to 42% of AI products and outcome pricing from 18% to 23% over six months; companies blend 1.7 pricing models on average. Not confirmed against the primary report. — [Falkster](https://falkster.com/blog/the-credit-is-the-new-seat); [Timberwolf Analytics](https://timberwolfanalytics.com/ai-pricing-2026/)
- **[survey, conflicting]** Orb's 2026 study of 80 AI agent companies put outcome-based pricing at 3.8% (down from 4.5%), far below ICONIQ's 23%; samples and definitions differ. TrulyCritic's 2026 review of 92 tools: pure per-seat 15% (from 21%), hybrid seat-plus-usage 41% (from 27%). — [Revenue Wizards](https://revenuewizards.com/blog/ai-pricing-models-credit-costs); [SimpleKPI](https://www.simplekpi.com/blog/the-end-of-seat-pricing)
- **[company]** GitHub Copilot replaced premium request units with token-metered "GitHub AI Credits" from 1 June 2026 (announced 27 April 2026). Seat prices unchanged, with an equal dollar amount of credits included: Business $19/user/month, Enterprise $39, Pro $10, Pro+ $39. Credits are drawn down at published per-model API rates (input, output and cached tokens). Existing Business/Enterprise customers received promotional credits June-August. — [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/); [GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026/04/27/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/)
- **[reported]** Reception of the Copilot change: when credits run out the service stops; heavy users expect higher bills; whether it is a price increase depends on usage. — [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/github-copilot-token-billing/); [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/software/development/github-copilot-pricing-changes-usage-based-billing-explained)
- **[company/reported]** Intercom Fin: $0.99 per resolved conversation (minimum 50 outcomes a month standalone); a sales lead qualification outcome costs $9.99. Fin is reported at or near $100M ARR, roughly half of Intercom's revenue (sources disagree on timing). Intercom's CFO said resolution rate rose from about 25% at launch to 65-70%; a vendor figure is 76% across 12,000+ customers. — [Subscription Playbook case study](https://www.subscriptionplaybook.com/case-studies/intercom-fin-per-resolution-pricing/); [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/ai-pulse/ai-pulse-2026-08-02-intercom-s-fin-ai-agent-is-nearing-100m-arr-roughly-half-of/); [Pluno](https://pluno.ai/blog/intercom-fin-ai-pricing)
- **[reported, unverified]** Two secondary sources say Salesforce agreed to buy Intercom for about $3.6B in 2026 (attributed to MarTech). Not confirmed against primary reporting. — [Aissist](https://aissist.io/insights/intercom-fin-review)
- **[reported]** HubSpot moved its agents to outcome pricing in April 2026, billed in credits. — [DEV Community, "The Great Repricing"](https://dev.to/blogwithsumit/the-great-repricing-how-b2b-saas-is-charging-for-ai-in-2026-real-data-1hoc)
- **[company]** Cursor's 2025 switch from request counts to dollar-denominated usage (section 2) is the reference case of flat "unlimited" pricing failing once agents do the work. — [FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/magazine/articles/cursor-pricing-change-user-backlash-refund)
- **[forecast]** IDC expects pure seat-based pricing to be largely obsolete by 2028, with 70% of vendors refactoring pricing around consumption or outcomes. — [SimpleKPI](https://www.simplekpi.com/blog/the-end-of-seat-pricing)
- **[opinion]** TechCrunch's Marina Temkin: annualising one month of revenue "can be misleading" because so many AI companies charge on usage. — [Michael Hochstat, "ARR is not defined"](https://hochstat.substack.com/p/arr-is-not-defined)

### Inferences
- Effect per model: flat seats give predictable revenue and unpredictable cost (margin risk sits with the vendor); credits/usage give protected margin and unpredictable revenue (risk sits with the customer, and "ARR" becomes a run rate); outcome pricing gives the vendor both the cost risk and the quality risk, so it only works where the resolution rate is high and measurable, as at Fin.
- Copilot's design (seat price equals included credits at API list rates) implies the seat itself carries little margin on the included usage; margin must come from breakage and overage.
- The more usage-based the pricing, the less a headline "ARR" says about contracted recurring revenue (links to section 6).

### Gaps
- Kyle Poyar's and ICONIQ's primary pages were not opened; percentages come from secondary summaries.
- No data found on realised margin by pricing model.
- Fin's gross margin per resolution is undisclosed.

## 5. Which notable AI product companies failed, shut down, were acqui-hired or collapsed in 2024-2026, and why?

### Takeaway
The documented failures fall into a few repeating causes: inference cost far above revenue (Sora), no retained demand (Humane, Yupp, many consumer apps), misstated revenue (Builder.ai), displacement by the labs' own products (Jasper), and legal/safety liabilities (Character.AI). Many "exits" were reverse acqui-hires that paid the team and left the product behind.

### Cited Findings
- **[reported, Oct 2026]** Character.AI filed for bankruptcy in October 2026, five years after launch; Google had paid $2.7B in 2024 in a deal NPR describes as a "reverse acquihire"; the company faced litigation over alleged harm to minors and copyright concerns from Disney. — [NPR Planet Money, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.npr.org/sections/planet-money/2026/10/16/nx-s1-6001828/what-this-bankruptcy-says-about-ai-startups)
- **[reported, 2026]** OpenAI shut down the Sora app in 2026, with high operating cost and poor retention cited. Forbes, citing Cantor Fitzgerald analyst Deepak Mathivanan, estimated peak inference cost at about $15M per day against $2.1M lifetime in-app revenue (Appfigures); a WSJ-attributed figure is about $1M per day. Dates conflict: 24 March 2026 versus web/app end on 26 April 2026 and API on 24 September 2026. — [TechCrunch AI graveyard](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/the-ai-graveyard-a-running-list-of-projects-and-startups-that-didnt-make-it/); [ngram](https://www.ngram.com/blog/openai-sora-shutdown-ai-video-economics); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/sora-was-reportedly-costing-openai-usd1-million-per-day)
- **[reported, 2026]** TechCrunch's running list (14 Nov 2026) also includes: ChatGPT Atlas discontinued in August 2026 after less than a year; audio app Huxe shut in May 2026; workflow tool Relay shut in August 2026; Yupp shut in March 2026 for lack of product-market fit. — [TechCrunch AI graveyard](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/the-ai-graveyard-a-running-list-of-projects-and-startups-that-didnt-make-it/); [Emergent summary](https://emergent.sh/news/ai-graveyard-projects-startups-failed-2026)
- **[reported, May 2025]** Builder.ai (Microsoft-backed, valued about $1.5B) entered insolvency after an internal investigation found revenue inflated by as much as 300%: 2024 revenue restated from $220M to about $55M (one source: $51M) and 2023 from $180M to $45M. ## 6. What do critical analyses say about ARR inflation, circular financing and bubble risk?

### Takeaway
Critics document three ways headline AI revenue overstates money earned: annualising a short or best period under usage pricing, counting pilots/one-off/unactivated contracts as recurring, and ignoring churn and credits. Separately, regulators and investors flag circular financing among Nvidia, OpenAI, Oracle and hyperscalers as a systemic risk; defenders point to real cash flow at Nvidia.

### Cited Findings
- **[reported, Sept 2025]** Fortune: six VCs said founders count pilots, one-time deals and unactivated contracts as recurring revenue; some claim "booked ARR" based on future payments customers can often opt out of. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/09/28/how-is-arr-calculated-startups-venture/)
- **[opinion, May 2025]** Slow Ventures' Will Quist: many companies raise on "vibe revenue ... pilots being counted as revenue, really terrible retention". — [TBPN digest](https://www.tbpndigest.com/story/2025-05-09/slow-ventures-will-quist-on-why-most-ai-startups-are-selling-vibe-revenue-and-the-case-for-capitalist-founders)
- **[opinion]** Run-rate figures under usage pricing are momentum indicators, not contracted recurring revenue; multiplying one month by 12 can overstate durability. — [Michael Hochstat, "ARR is not defined"](https://hochstat.substack.com/p/arr-is-not-defined)
- **[reported, April 2026]** Forbes: seed-stage AI startups "are flashing record revenue numbers and most of them are not what they seem" (cites ChartMogul retention data). — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/04/08/seed-stage-ai-startups-are-flashing-record-revenue-numbers-and-most-of-them-are-not-what-they-seem/)
- **[analysis]** A 2026 private-company analysis of Replit notes the $525M ARR figure is gross, not net of churn, not adjusted for promotional credits and not confirmed by audited financials; other estimates for Replit range from $150M (modelled) to $265M (end 2025) to about $300M (end 2025). — [QuantLogix](https://quantlogix.ai/research-library/th-replit-2026-09-03); [North Metric](https://www.northmetric.io/estimate/replit); [Clinkbill](https://clinkbill.com/arr-leaderboard/replit)
- **[verified case]** Builder.ai is the documented extreme: revenue restated to about a quarter of the claimed figure (section 5). — [TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/unicorn-builder-ai-collapses-fomo-investing/)
- **[report, Aug 2025; contested]** MIT NANDA report: 95% of enterprise generative AI pilots delivered no measurable return; sample reported by Fortune as 150 executive interviews, 350 employees surveyed, 300 projects. Criticisms: the 95% refers on a funnel chart to custom task-specific tools reaching production, not all pilots; about 80% of companies never piloted such tools (denominator problem); failure defined as no sustained profit gain; six-month window; self-selected sample; NANDA is an agent-infrastructure project with a possible interest (Fortune adds no evidence of skew). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/21/an-mit-report-that-95-of-ai-pilots-fail-spooked-investors-but-the-reason-why-those-pilots-failed-is-what-should-make-the-c-suite-anxious/); [80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/ai-workplace-mit-study/); [HPCwire/BigDATAwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/2025/09/04/mit-report-flags-95-genai-failure-rate-but-critics-say-it-oversimplifies/)
- **[reported]** Circular financing: Nvidia pledged up to $100B to OpenAI, which committed to Nvidia chips; OpenAI reportedly agreed to buy about $300B of Oracle compute over five years from 2027; Oracle buys Nvidia chips to build it. One analysis puts Nvidia's investments in its own customers at about $110B and says it guaranteed up to $105B of residual value on OpenAI's Ohio data-centre project. — [Fortune, Sept 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/09/28/nvidia-openai-circular-financing-ai-bubble); [Luminix](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/industry-analysis/ai-circular-deals); [US News](https://money.usnews.com/investing/articles/will-circular-financing-pop-the-ai-bubble)
- **[reported]** The IMF and the BIS both identified AI-related circular financing as a systemic downside risk; by mid-2026 CDS market participants reportedly treated Nvidia's spread as a barometer of AI vendor-financing stress. — [Wikipedia, "AI build-out financing"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_build-out_financing) (secondary; check IMF/BIS originals)
- **[opinion]** Michael Burry: AI is "a circular financing web with Nvidia in the middle"; claims five hyperscalers carry $1.65 trillion of off-balance-sheet debt. Americans for Financial Reform: "the AI bubble is primed to pop". — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/michael-burry-sounds-alarm-again-144154784.html); [AFR press release](https://ourfinancialsecurity.org/news/press-release-circular-financing-arrangements-mounting-risky-debt-and-increased-stock-market-exposure-the-ai-bubble-is-primed-to-pop/)
- **[counter-view]** Jensen Huang called the circular-financing label "ridiculous"; Nvidia generated close to $48B of free cash flow in a recent quarter; one analysis concludes no single deal confirms a financing bubble. — [Vantage Markets](https://www.vantagemarkets.com/en/market-analysis/ai-financing-bubble-nvidia-openai-circular-deals-explained/)
- **[opinion — Ed Zitron]** "Subprime AI crisis" (term used since September 2024): labs sell below cost and will have to raise prices; claims, from sourced AWS billing data, that Anthropic's AWS compute spend exceeds 100% of revenue; "Running Dry" (2026) argues Anthropic's Q2 2026 numbers reflected artificially depressed costs. Mostly paywalled; only headlines/summaries seen. Rebuttal that costs can be controlled in The New Stack. — [Where's Your Ed At](https://www.wheresyoured.at/anthropic-and-openai-have-begun-the-subprime-ai-crisis/); [Running Dry](https://wheresyoured.at/running-dry); [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/how-solid-is-ed-zitrons-case-against-generative-ai/?p=22802486)

### Inferences
- A checklist to separate "high revenue" from "money earned": Is it contracted or a run rate? Which month was annualised? Are pilots, credits or free promotional usage included? What is GRR after 12 months? What is gross margin after inference? Is the buyer also an investor or supplier?
- Circular financing matters to application companies indirectly: if lab funding tightens, subsidised token prices and generous rate limits are the first things to change, which hits app margins (section 2).
- The MIT 95% figure is widely quoted but weakly supported; it should be cited as contested, not as fact.

### Gaps
- Most critical ARR reporting found is from 2025; little primary 2026 reporting on ARR practices surfaced apart from Forbes (April 2026).
- IMF and BIS statements were not checked at source.
- No quantified estimate of how much reported AI ARR is pilots or credits.

## 7. Which companies are demonstrably profitable and what do they have in common?

### Takeaway
The companies with the best evidence of profit (Midjourney, Surge AI, Gamma, ElevenLabs) share little or no venture dependence at the start, small teams, direct paid demand from day one, and control over their main cost (own models, or a service where the customer pays for labour). "Demonstrably" is relative: all evidence is company statements or press estimates, none audited.

### Cited Findings
- **[reported]** Midjourney: no outside funding; about $300M revenue in fiscal 2024 (Forbes citing PitchBook) and described as profitable by Forbes and its CEO; roughly 60 staff by one estimate (about $5M revenue per employee). A different source estimates $500-600M for 2026. — [AIBusiness.vc leaderboard](https://aibusiness.vc/startups/ai-revenue-leaderboard); [New Market Pitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-infrastructure-revenue-per-employee)
- **[reported]** Surge AI: bootstrapped to roughly $1B revenue in 2024 (Forbes: about $1.2B); founder Edwin Chen says profitable from nearly day one; one tracker cites $1.4B annualised as of August 2025 per Reuters. Customer base concentrated in AI labs. — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/surge-ai); [Tanay Jaipuria on X](https://x.com/tanayj/status/2008284872343708061?lang=en); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surge_AI)
- **[company]** Gamma: says it has been profitable since 2023; reached $100M ARR as of November 2025 per the company. — [AIBusiness.vc leaderboard](https://aibusiness.vc/startups/ai-revenue-leaderboard)
- **[reported/estimate]** ElevenLabs: about $500M ARR in April 2026 and about $600M by September 2026 (CEO, per one tracker); Forbes reportedly estimated about $116M profit in 2025; the company has not disclosed margins or EBITDA. — [AI Business Weekly](https://aibusinessweekly.net/p/elevenlabs-statistics); [Clinkbill](https://clinkbill.com/arr-leaderboard/elevenlabs)
- **[estimate]** Photoroom: rated "likely profitable" by one guide; exact earnings undisclosed. — [New Market Pitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-infrastructure-profitable-startups)
- **[survey]** Bessemer's "Shooting Stars" (about 60% gross margin, slower but durable growth) are the benchmark group closest to this profile, as opposed to "Supernovas" at about 25% margin. — [Decasonic](https://www.decasonic.com/perspectives/from-narrative-premium-to-proof-premium)
- **[survey]** ChartMogul: AI products priced above $250/month retain at 70% GRR / 85% NRR, i.e. like normal B2B SaaS. — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/)
- **[reported]** Within loss-making leaders, the profitable part is the enterprise segment: Cursor positive on enterprise, negative on individuals (April 2026); Replit's CEO claims 80-90% enterprise margins. — [Tech Economics Substack](https://techeconomics.substack.com/p/the-60-billion-company-with-negative); [Wing VC](https://www.wing.vc/content/who-makes-money-when-inference-gets-10x-cheaper)

### Inferences
- Common traits: (1) own model or own cost base, so no margin paid to a competitor-supplier (Midjourney, ElevenLabs); or a labour/service business where AI labs are the customer rather than the supplier (Surge); (2) paid from the first user, no free tier subsidy as growth engine (Midjourney); (3) small headcount and high revenue per employee; (4) a narrow, repeated job with a finished output (image, voice, deck, labelled data) rather than open-ended agent usage; (5) bootstrapped or lightly funded at the start, so pricing had to cover cost.
- Surge's profile carries concentration risk: its customers are the labs whose spending depends on the financing discussed in section 6.
- Selling to the builders (data, voice infrastructure) and selling a finished output have been more reliably profitable than reselling general model access under a flat fee.

### Gaps
- No audited profit figure for any company listed; ElevenLabs' profit is a press estimate, Photoroom's status is an aggregator opinion.
- No 2026 profit confirmation found for Midjourney or Surge AI; latest solid figures are for 2024.
- No systematic list of profitable bootstrapped AI companies with verified numbers was found.
