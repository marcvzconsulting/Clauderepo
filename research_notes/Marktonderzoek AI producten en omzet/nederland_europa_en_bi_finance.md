## A2. How far is AI adoption among Dutch and EU companies, especially SMEs, and what do they spend on?

### Takeaway
Official statistics show adoption is real but far from saturated: 20% of EU enterprises (10+ staff) used AI in 2025, the Netherlands sits just behind the Nordic leaders, large firms are at roughly 55–65% and Dutch micro-businesses at 13.8%. Surveys with looser definitions report 74–78% of SMEs "using AI in some form", which mostly means employees using general chat tools, with almost no policy around it.

### Cited Findings
- EU: 20.0% of enterprises with 10+ employees used AI technologies in 2025, up from 13.5% in 2024 (VERIFIED official statistic) — [Eurostat news, 11 Dec 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20251211-2)
- EU by size 2025: small 17%, medium 30.36%, large 55.03%. Highest: Denmark 42%, Finland 37.8%, Sweden 35%; lowest: Romania 5.2%, Poland 8.4%, Bulgaria 8.5% — [Eurostat Statistics Explained](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php/Use_of_artificial_intelligence_in_enterprises)
- Netherlands: Benelux countries follow directly behind Denmark, Sweden and Finland in AI use — [CBS, Digitalisering en kenniseconomie 2025](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/longread/rapportages/2026/digitalisering-en-kenniseconomie-2025/3-ict-gebruik-bij-bedrijven)
- Netherlands 2025, companies with 10+ staff: search summaries give two different CBS-attributed figures — "about a third" and "one in six, double two years ago". Not reconciled; needs the StatLine table — [CBS news 2025/50](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/nieuws/2025/50/bedrijven-gebruiken-ai-vaakst-voor-marketing-of-verkoop); [Accountant.nl](https://www.accountant.nl/nieuws/2025/12/bedrijven-gebruiken-ai-vooral-voor-marketing-verkoop-en-administratie/)
- Netherlands, 250+ staff: 65% use AI — [CBS news 2025/44](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/nieuws/2025/44/bedrijven-die-ai-gebruiken-zijn-vaak-groter) **[mapping uncertain]**
- Netherlands, micro-businesses (2–10 staff): 13.8% used AI in 2025 (CBS report, March 2026) — [CBS microbedrijven](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/longread/rapportages/2026/gebruik-van-ai-technologie-door-nederlandse-microbedrijven/2-gebruik-ai-technologie); [Accountant.nl](https://www.accountant.nl/nieuws/2026/3/microbedrijven-blijven-nog-achter-met-gebruik-van-ai/)
- Purpose: Dutch companies use AI most often for marketing or sales, followed by administration — [CBS news 2025/50](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/nieuws/2025/50/bedrijven-gebruiken-ai-vaakst-voor-marketing-of-verkoop); [Accountant.nl](https://www.accountant.nl/nieuws/2025/12/bedrijven-gebruiken-ai-vooral-voor-marketing-verkoop-en-administratie/)
- ABN AMRO / MWM2 survey (June 2026, 777 Dutch organisations): 78% of SMEs use AI in some form; 9% have formal policy for external AI tools; 26% have a formal incident-response plan — [Computable](https://www.computable.nl/2026/06/24/abn-amro-mkb-gebruikt-ai-maar-ontbeert-beleid-voor-relevante-risicos/)
- 84% of Dutch SMEs say they want to invest more in AI over the next three years; a Sharp survey puts broad use at up to 74% (commercial site summarising surveys; "ambition high, use low") — [AI Platform MKB](https://www.aiplatformmkb.nl/achtergrond/ai-in-mkb-stand-van-zaken-juni-2026)
- Visma (2.4M customers, strong in Dutch SME software): all strategic national platforms now have built-in AI functions, and customers with access to them represent 40% of ARR (SELF-CLAIMED, H1 2026) — [Analist.nl](https://www.analist.nl/corporatenieuws/1471033-visma-zet-sterke-groei-voort-in-eerste-helft-van-2026)

### Inferences
- The difference between ~14–33% (CBS/Eurostat: AI technologies deployed in the business) and ~75% (bank surveys: any use) is the opportunity zone: staff use chat tools, but few SMEs have AI built into a process such as reporting or forecasting.
- SMEs appear to receive AI mainly bundled inside software they already pay for (Visma's 40%-of-ARR figure), which implies low willingness to pay for stand-alone AI and higher willingness to pay for outcomes delivered inside existing tools.
- The policy gap (9% with formal policy) is itself a service opportunity: governance, safe set-up, and "which data may go where".

### Gaps
- No reliable figure for what Dutch SMEs actually spend on AI (per company or in total). KVK, ING, Rabobank, TNO and AWVN reports were not found by the searches; McKinsey not searched.
- CBS detail (purposes by percentage, bought vs. built, reasons for non-use among micro-businesses) could not be read: www.cbs.nl is blocked in this session and must be allowed in the network settings to complete this.
- The CBS 10+ staff figure for 2025 is unresolved (one third vs. one in six).

## A3. What do the EU AI Act, GDPR/AVG and data-sovereignty concerns mean for selling AI products in the Netherlands?

### Takeaway
For a BI/finance AI product the regulatory burden in late 2026 is light: the high-risk obligations were postponed to December 2027, and what applies now is mainly transparency (Article 50) plus the existing AVG. Sovereignty is a live concern in Dutch government, healthcare and increasingly SMEs, and there is evidence of dependence on US providers but little evidence yet of paid switching.

### Cited Findings
- The Digital Omnibus (cited as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) entered into force on 27 July 2026; stand-alone Annex III high-risk obligations moved to 2 December 2027, and AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I) to 2 August 2028 — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Obligara, 13 Nov 2026](https://obligara.com/news/2026-11-13-eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-delay)
- Article 50 transparency duties were not postponed and stay on the 2 August 2026 schedule; only watermarking for already-deployed systems has a grace period to 2 December 2026 — [Cloud Security Alliance](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/); [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html) (the latter predates entry into force)
- The Omnibus also added prohibitions and labelling deadlines dated 2 December 2026 — [Future AGI](https://futureagi.com/blog/eu-ai-act-timeline-digital-omnibus-2027/); [Planet Crust](https://www.planetcrust.com/eu-ai-act-delay-what-still-applies/)
- NOS analysis of 16,500 domain names of government, healthcare and vital companies: 67% linked to US cloud services — [IO+](https://ioplus.nl/nl/posts/waarom-we-niet-uit-de-amerikaanse-digitale-houdgreep-komen)
- Almost half of Dutch companies are partly or heavily dependent on US cloud and AI providers; smaller companies have more often already partly switched to European cloud/software than large ones (underlying study and year not verified) — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/mkb-wendbaarder-grootbedrijf-digitale-afhankelijkheid)
- Local government: only 39.3% fully European-hosted and operated — [Binnenlands Bestuur](https://www.binnenlandsbestuur.nl/digitaal/hoe-gemeenten-grip-op-hun-data-verliezen)
- Nextcloud sovereignty index: Netherlands 36.3 vs. Finland 64.5 and Germany 53.8 (vendor-sponsored index) — [MT/Sprout](https://mtsprout.nl/tech-innovatie/digitale-soevereiniteit-nextcloud-onderzoek)
- EY: European sovereign alternatives are growing but not yet equivalent to US public-cloud suites in the short term, especially on integration — [EY Nederland](https://www.ey.com/nl_nl/insights/consulting/digitale-soevereiniteit-in-de-publieke-sector)
- Dutch government acknowledges the need but has no uniform principles defining cloud sovereignty; AWS European Sovereign Cloud is assessed as meeting only part of the requirements, with US access to data "unlikely but not inconceivable" — [Tweede Kamer document 2026D34381](https://www.tweedekamer.nl/downloads/document?id=2026D34381) **[mapping uncertain]**
- Juvoly/Tandem explicitly kept patient data on Dutch servers after the acquisition, i.e. local hosting is used as a selling point in Dutch healthcare — [Zorginnovatie.nl](https://zorginnovatie.nl/nieuws/juvoly-sluit-zich-aan-bij-europese-marktleider-tandem-health)
- AFAS only allows external AI through certified integrations; as of June 2026 none was certified (secondary source) — [Bombos kennisbank](https://bombos.ai/kennisbank/welk-boekhoudpakket-heeft-een-ai-assistent/) **[mapping uncertain]**

### Inferences
- Internal financial reporting, forecasting and dashboard commentary are not in the Annex III high-risk categories discussed in these sources, so a BI/finance tool mainly needs: AI-disclosure where users interact with it, an AVG-compliant processing agreement, EU data residency, and a clear statement on model providers. (Credit scoring of natural persons and HR decisions would be different — legal check needed; not verified here.)
- "Compliant, Dutch-language, EU-hosted, runs inside your own Microsoft tenant" is a credible differentiator against US point tools, especially for municipalities, healthcare and accountants. Since Power BI itself is Microsoft, the pragmatic sovereignty pitch is "no data leaves your existing tenant", not "no US vendor".
- The policy vacuum in SMEs (see A2) plus Article 50 gives a consultant a concrete, low-cost add-on: AI-use policy and transparency set-up bundled with delivery.

### Gaps
- The Official Journal text of the Omnibus was not checked; the watermarking grace period and the 2 Dec 2026 prohibitions come from law-firm and vendor blogs.
- No Dutch data on customers actually paying a premium for sovereign or Dutch-language AI.
- Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens guidance on AI/LLM use (2026) was not searched.

## A4. Which Dutch-specific niches are served by AI products (accounting software, legal, healthcare reporting, municipalities)?

### Takeaway
Dutch bookkeeping platforms are adding AI in two ways: built-in assistants (AFAS "Jonas", Visma platform AI) and open connections that let customers bring their own AI (Moneybird via MCP). Healthcare documentation is the most mature Dutch AI niche. The management-reporting layer on top of bookkeeping data is where vendors are still vague.

### Cited Findings
- Moneybird did not build its own assistant; it opened an MCP connection (available to all users since Sept 2025) so external AI tools can read, or read and write (not delete), the administration; the customer pays for their own AI subscription, not for the connection — [Softwarepakketten.nl](https://www.softwarepakketten.nl/bericht/8436&bronw=1/Moneybird_lanceert_AI_koppeling_via_MCP.htm); [Boekhoudvergelijker](https://boekhoudvergelijker.com/boekhoudprogramma-met-ai-welke-pakketten-gebruiken-kunstmatige-intelligentie/)
- AFAS has its own assistant "Jonas"; Lefebvre Sdu's legal/tax AI is being integrated with it (reported 1 Dec 2025) — [Softwarepakketten.nl archive](https://www.softwarepakketten.nl/cmm/berichten/berichtenarchief_raadpl_cat.php?cat=389&bronw=7&pg=263&ar=mn) **[mapping uncertain]**
- AFAS CEO Bas van der Veldt: two customers recently cancelled part of their subscription because they will build that part themselves with AI (column, Oct 2026) — [MT/Sprout](https://mtsprout.nl/columnisten-experts/ai-snoept-nu-al-omzet-af-van-afas-en-daar-is-ceo-bas-van-der-veldt-best-blij-mee)
- Visma merged Nmbrs, Yuki, WeFact and Visionplanner into one Dutch business platform on 1 Sept 2026, citing AI as a reason; an observer notes it is unclear how much of the announced AI an entrepreneur can use today — [Floh Solutions](https://www.floh.solutions/kennis/signaal/boekhouding-wordt-ai-grens)
- Visma group: revenue just over EUR 3.1B in 2025, 2.4M customers (+>20%); H1 2026 revenue EUR 1,615M, +18.8% (REPORTED from company results) — [Accountancy Vanmorgen](https://www.accountancyvanmorgen.nl/2026/03/19/omzet-visma-boven-3-miljard-klantenbestand-explodeert/); [Computable](https://www.computable.nl/2026/08/12/visma-rondt-kaap-van-3-miljard-euro-aan-jaarlijkse-omzet/)
- Healthcare reporting: Juvoly/Tandem Health (GPs), Autoscriber, Medendo, ValueCare (GGZ) — see A1 — [Het Laatste AI Nieuws](https://www.hetlaatsteainieuws.nl/achtergrond/ai-in-de-huisartsenpraktijk-2026)

### Inferences
- Visionplanner (Dutch SME reporting/forecasting, now inside Visma) and the MCP opening at Moneybird show two things at once: incumbents are absorbing the "reporting on top of bookkeeping" layer, and the data is becoming accessible to third parties and to customers' own AI.
- The AFAS anecdote (customers replacing modules with self-built AI) is early evidence that mid-sized Dutch firms will build small internal tools rather than buy; this favours selling expertise, templates and semantic models over selling thin software.
- For someone with Power BI and finance skills, the reachable niche is the layer the bookkeeping vendors serve poorly: multi-entity management reporting, variance commentary and forecasts for SMEs and for accountancy firms advising them.

### Gaps
- Nothing found on Exact (AI features, revenue), on Dutch legal AI products, or on municipal AI adoption (GPT-NL, vendor names, budgets).
- No user or revenue numbers for the AI features of AFAS, Exact, Moneybird or Yuki.

## B1. AI products around Power BI / Fabric / Excel and "chat with your data": what exists, what has traction, how good is Copilot, what gaps remain?

### Takeaway
Microsoft Fabric is a large, fast-growing base (40,000+ paid customers, >USD 2B run-rate) but Copilot in Power BI is capacity-priced, needs a carefully prepared semantic model, and is non-deterministic. The most important 2026 shift is that Microsoft opened Power BI to external AI agents through MCP servers, so general agents (Claude, ChatGPT etc.) can now build and query models; independent "chat with your data" tools have modest or undisclosed revenue.

### Cited Findings

**Microsoft**
- Fabric: >40,000 paid customers, up >60% y/y (Q4 FY26 call, 29 July 2026); 35,000 at Q3 FY26 (29 Apr 2026); annual revenue run-rate "over USD 2 billion", +60% y/y, with >31,000 customers (Q2 FY26, Jan 2026) (SELF-CLAIMED on earnings calls, i.e. management statements by a listed company) — [Microsoft FY26 Q4 earnings](https://microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4); [Motley Fool Q3 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/29/microsoft-msft-q3-2026-earnings-transcript/); [Yahoo Finance Q2 highlights](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/microsoft-q2-earnings-call-highlights-010010532.html)
- Copilot in Power BI is paid through Fabric or Premium capacity, not through a Microsoft 365 Copilot seat; heavy prompting competes with other workloads and can contribute to throttling — [AI Agent Square](https://aiagentsquare.com/agents/power-bi-copilot); [Beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/microsoft-fabric-copilot-pricing-capacity-units-per-token-fabric-copilot-capacity-throttling-2026)
- Microsoft is retiring the stand-alone Power BI Copilot experiences in favour of Microsoft Copilot (Preview), with defaults changing on 8 Dec 2026 — [Power BI November 2026 Feature Summary](https://community.fabric.microsoft.com/blog/fbc_pbiupdatesblog/power-bi-november-2026-feature-summary/5414287)

## B3. Where are the unmet needs for SMEs and consultants, and what would customers pay?

### Takeaway
The evidence points to a gap between cheap bookkeeping-embedded AI and USD 15K+ FP&A suites: automated management reporting with narrative commentary, cash-flow forecasting and AI-ready data models for companies too small for Pigment/Datarails and without Fabric capacity. Direct willingness-to-pay data was not found; price anchors come from adjacent tools.

### Cited Findings
- Copilot requires Fabric/Premium capacity rather than a per-user seat, so Pro-only organisations lack Microsoft's own AI in Power BI — [AI Agent Square](https://aiagentsquare.com/agents/power-bi-copilot)
- Copilot output quality depends on semantic-model preparation (AI data schema, verified answers, AI instructions), which the customer must do — [Datastudios guide](https://www.datastudios.org/post/how-to-prep-power-bi-semantic-model-for-copilot-schema-verified-answers-instructions); [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/create-reports/copilot-prepare-data-ai-instructions)

- Price anchors: Agicap EUR 150–799/month (reported by a review site) and criticised as expensive for small firms; Cube ~USD 15K–45K/year; DataSnipper ~USD 64/user/month (third-party estimate) — [Audit Friendly](https://www.auditfriendly.co/software/agicap); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/196637/Agicap/); [CFO Shortlist](https://www.cfoshortlist.com/reports/cube-alternatives-2026); [Blast Audit](https://blast-audit.com/blog/datasnipper-price-increase-options/)

- Dutch companies use AI mostly for marketing/sales and administration (finance and reporting are not the leading use) — [CBS news 2025/50](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/nieuws/2025/50/bedrijven-gebruiken-ai-vaakst-voor-marketing-of-verkoop)
- Moneybird exposes administrations to external AI through MCP, so SME bookkeeping data is technically reachable by third-party reporting tools — [Softwarepakketten.nl](https://www.softwarepakketten.nl/bericht/8436&bronw=1/Moneybird_lanceert_AI_koppeling_via_MCP.htm)

### Inferences (no direct source; reasoning from the above)
- Candidate offers, in order of fit with a Power BI/finance profile:
  1. Monthly management report with written variance commentary, generated from the bookkeeping system into Power BI or a web page (price anchor: below Agicap, e.g. low hundreds of euros per month per entity — an assumption, not measured).
  2. Cash-flow and scenario forecasting for SMEs that find Agicap too expensive.
  3. "AI-ready semantic model" service: documentation, descriptions, AI instructions, verified answers, permission review — sold as fixed-price package to organisations switching Copilot on.
  4. Data-model documentation and DAX review using agents over PBIP/TMDL (now feasible via MCP), sold to BI teams and consultancies.
  5. Consolidation and multi-entity reporting for small groups, where bookkeeping packages are weak.
- Accountancy and advisory firms are a more efficient channel than individual SMEs: one sale covers many administrations, and they already buy tools such as DataSnipper and Visionplanner.
- A product that is only a thin chat layer over Power BI is at high risk from Microsoft and general agents (see B1); defensibility would come from finance content (models, driver logic, Dutch reporting conventions) and connectors.

### Gaps

- No demand-side evidence (search volumes, tender data, forum threads) was collected for consolidation or model documentation.

## B4. Is there a market for AI-assisted BI/finance consulting or productised services in the Netherlands (rates, demand)?

### Takeaway
Dutch Power BI freelance rates sit around EUR 70–100 per hour on average, with seniors at EUR 100–130, and clients offer noticeably less than freelancers ask, suggesting price pressure on pure hourly work. Demand signals for AI-specific BI services are indirect (Fabric growth, Copilot preparation needs, the SME policy gap); no direct measurement was found.

### Cited Findings
- Power BI specialist rates 2026: average EUR 70–100/hour; junior EUR 50–70, senior EUR 100–130 (architecture, security) (marketplace page, commercial source) — [Freelancer.nl](https://freelancer.nl/freelancers/power-bi-services)
- Across all disciplines, Dutch clients offer on average EUR 87/hour while freelancers ask EUR 106, a 22% gap (Freelance Markt Index Q2 2026) — [Freelance.nl](https://www.freelance.nl/community/freelancers-vragen-22-meer-dan-opdrachtgevers-bieden); [Freelance Markt Index Q2 2026 (PDF)](https://lp.freelance.nl/hubfs/Freelance%20Markt%20Index%20Q2%202026.pdf)

- Employed Power BI experts: EUR 2,800–5,000/month for 24–40 hours (one vacancy) — [YoungCapital](https://www.youngcapital.nl/vacatures/4196095-start-als-power-bi-expert-binnen-nederland)

### Inferences
- With a 22% ask/offer gap and agents able to write DAX and build models, hourly Power BI development is likely to commoditise; fixed-price packages (model audit, AI-readiness, monthly report-as-a-service) decouple income from hours and let the consultant keep the productivity gain from AI.
- At EUR 100–130/hour, a senior consultant who delivers in a day what used to take three can price a package at the old three-day value; that arbitrage exists only while clients still price on outcomes they recognise (a working report pack, a forecast).
- The combination finance + Power BI + AI is rarer than Power BI alone, and FP&A vendors' price levels (USD 15K–45K/year) give room for a recurring service priced well below them.

### Gaps
- No independent rate survey specific to BI/finance consultants in the Netherlands; the EUR 70–130 range is from a marketplace page.
- No data on demand for or pricing of productised BI/finance services (fixed-price dashboards, report subscriptions) in the Netherlands.
- No information on how Dutch BI consultancies have changed prices or staffing because of AI.
