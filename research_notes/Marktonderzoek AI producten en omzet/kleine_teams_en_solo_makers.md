# Small-team, solo-founder and bootstrapped AI products with demonstrable revenue (state: November 2026)

Method note for the report writer: research done 25 Nov 2026 via web search. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (indiehackers.com, techstartups.com, bigideasdb.com did not resolve), so nearly all figures below come from search-result extracts of the cited pages, not from a full read of the page. Where a figure's exact carrying page is uncertain, that is stated. Verification labels used throughout:

- **VERIFIED** = payment-provider-connected (TrustMRR/Stripe read-only) or confirmed by a counterparty (acquirer, Stripe customer story).
- **REPORTED** = stated by a reputable news outlet, usually relaying the company.
- **SELF-CLAIMED** = founder's own post/bio/dashboard, or an aggregator repeating it.
- **ESTIMATE** = third-party tracker (Latka, Sacra, Starter Story, arr.club) with no visible audit.

Survivorship bias warning: every named example below is a winner selected because it is talked about. The distribution data in question 2 is the corrective: most listed products earn little or nothing, and even those datasets over-represent products whose founders chose to connect Stripe publicly.

## 1. Which bootstrapped / solo / tiny-team AI products have verified or credible revenue in 2025–2026?

A few dozen well-known solo or sub-10-person AI products credibly earn $1M–$30M per year, but almost none of the headline numbers are independently verified: they are founder posts, X bios or tracker estimates. The best-documented cases (Chatbase via Stripe, Base44 and Cal AI via acquisitions) are also the ones that stopped being "tiny" or independent.

### Inferences

This is inferred from the case descriptions, not measured.
- Several of the famous image-generation products look flat or declining in 2026, while B2B (Chatbase) and tools-for-builders (TrustMRR, DataFast) grow.
- Time to scale in the winners was short (Base44 six months, Cal AI two years, Chatbase 2.5 years to $8M), which says more about selection than about what is typical.

- Few genuinely new 2026 names with verified revenue surfaced; the "new 2026 examples" lists found are blog listicles.
- Pricing per product was not collected.

## 2. What does the revenue distribution look like?

### Takeaway
On the only large payment-verified dataset (TrustMRR), roughly 2.4–2.7% of listed products reach $100k ARR; looser self-reported samples suggest about half of active indie products sit below $1k MRR. Small AI SaaS sold on marketplaces changes hands at about 3x revenue / 5x profit, far below venture-style AI multiples.

### Cited Findings
- TrustMRR snapshot September 2026: of 10,150 listed products, 272 (2.68%) had MRR at or above $8,333 ($100k ARR); a separate write-up gives about 2.4%. Covers listed products only and MRR measures subscriptions only — [TechStartups, 28 Sep 2026](https://techstartups.com/2026/09/28/what-percentage-of-indie-hacker-products-make-100k-in-arr/).
- TrustMRR states over 15,000 startups listed and over 3,000 for sale; a directory page showed 8,835 (possibly older) — [TrustMRR llms.txt](https://trustmrr.com/llms.txt); [TrustMRR directory](https://trustmrr.com/startups/page/7).
- TrustMRR verification is a read-only connection to the payment provider (Stripe, RevenueCat, Superwall, App Store, Creem) — [TrustMRR](https://www.trustmrr.com/). Caveat from a developer: it shows what was billed, not what was kept after refunds/chargebacks — [dev.to](https://dev.to/jason_01kk/i-integrated-trustmrrs-api-into-our-listing-flow-heres-the-verified-revenue-ecosystem-in-2026-57c3).
- Vendor estimate (approximate, community surveys plus own data): ~50% of active indie hackers under $1k/month, ~20% $1k–$10k, ~10% $10k–$100k, under 5% $100k+ — [BetterLaunch](https://www.betterlaunch.co/blog/indie-hacker).
- Scrape of 4,500 Indie Hackers products (self-reported MRR): ~29% at $100–$1k, ~26% at $1k–$10k, ~14% at $10k+; shares do not sum to 100% — [dev.to](https://dev.to/agenthustler/i-scraped-4500-indiehackers-products-heres-what-the-mrr-data-reveals-18ki).
- An Indie Hackers post analysed 5,079 Stripe-verified startups (contents not readable in this session) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-analyzed-5-079-stripe-verified-startups-f0f6bd053f).
- Stripe Atlas: solo founders were 63% of C corps formed via Atlas in Q2 2026 (all-time high). Median solo startup first-six-month revenue in 2025 fell 23% year over year while the top decile rose 19%; top decile earned about 61x the median — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/top-solo-founder-traits).
- Stripe Atlas: at two years, AI-native solo startups generated almost twice the revenue of other solo startups; median solo B2B revenue at month 24 was more than 4x median solo B2C; international sales were 51% of revenue for top-decile solo founders versus 2% for the median — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/top-solo-founder-traits).
- Stripe Atlas: 42% of Atlas founders said they were building AI startups in 2025 (15% in Jan 2023, 33% in 2024); 20% charged a first customer within 30 days in 2025 (8% in 2020) — [Stripe year in review](https://stripe.com/blog/stripe-atlas-startups-in-2025-year-in-review).
- Marketplace multiples, June 2026 analysis of 615 Acquire.com listings: overall median ~2.0x trailing revenue and 3.4x trailing profit; AI listings (n=22) 3.05x revenue, 5.10x profit — [BigIdeasDB acquisitions](https://bigideasdb.com/state-of-saas-acquisitions-2026).
- Second analysis of 651 listings: AI listings (n=28) 2.8x revenue, 4.95x profit, median asking price $192,500 — [BigIdeasDB valuations](https://bigideasdb.com/state-of-saas-valuations-2026).
- Acquire.com closed deals at a median 3.9x profit (SDE) in 2024 and 2025 for sub-$10M enterprise values — [Acquiry](https://www.acquiry.com/saas-valuation-multiples-2026/).
- For contrast, larger AI-native M&A is cited at 8–15x revenue — [SaaSRise](https://saasrise.com/blog/the-ai-software-valuation-report-2026).

### Inferences
- A median AI listing asking $192,500 at ~2.8x revenue implies trailing revenue around $70k/year (~$5.7k/month). This is my arithmetic on asking prices, not a reported median revenue.
- The share above $1k MRR is not reliably known. Self-selected samples put it at 40–50%; the TrustMRR $100k ARR share (about 2.5%) is the hardest number available. The true share across all launched AI products is certainly lower than any of these, because products that never earned anything are rarely listed.
- Stripe's data shows widening inequality: more solo founders, lower median, higher top decile.

### Gaps
- No direct, verified share of AI products above $1k and $10k MRR; TrustMRR category-level distribution could not be pulled (site/API not reachable).
- No official Acquire.com or Flippa 2026 report with AI medians found; the figures are third-party analyses with small AI samples (22–28).
- Flippa data not found.

## 3. Which niches work best for small players, and which are saturated?

### Takeaway
The evidence favours B2B and narrow workflow products over consumer novelty: Stripe's data shows median solo B2B revenue above 4x B2C at two years, and consumer AI mobile apps monetise well but churn hard. Headshot/photo generation is visibly crowded with small verified earners.

### Cited Findings
- Median solo B2B founder revenue at month 24 was more than four times the median solo B2C founder — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/top-solo-founder-traits).
- AI mobile subscription apps earn 41% more revenue per payer than non-AI apps but retain only 21.1% of annual subscribers after 12 months versus 30.7% for non-AI (RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026, 115,000+ apps, $16B+ revenue) — [TechCrunch, 10 Mar 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows); [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/).
- Mobile subscription can still produce the largest small-team outcome: Cal AI, 7 employees, >$30M revenue in two years — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens).
- AI headshots: several look-alike apps appear on TrustMRR with small verified revenue (e.g. "ai pro portrait" ranked #509; an AI video tool at $1.6k and an AI text-to-speech listing at $417 in 30-day revenue) alongside the leaders Aragon and HeadshotPro — [TrustMRR ai-pro-portrait](https://trustmrr.com/startup/ai-pro-portrait); [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/trustmrr).
- B2B support chatbots: Chatbase reached $10M ARR bootstrapped; an Indie Hackers teardown describes a "mini Chatbase" at $5.8k MRR for sale — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/customers/chatbase); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/chatbase-just-crossed-10m-arr-bootstrapped-here-is-a-teardown-of-a-5-8k-mrr-mini-chatbase-hidden-on-the-market-83e52469c7).
- Tools sold to other builders (revenue database, analytics, courses, boilerplates) make up all of Marc Lou's ~$80–98k/month — [yespress.io](https://yespress.io/marc-lou).
- A Google VP warned in Feb 2026 that thin LLM wrappers and aggregators may not survive, while deep vertical products (Cursor, Harvey cited) are better placed — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/21/google-vp-warns-that-two-types-of-ai-startups-may-not-survive).

### Inferences
- Saturated or eroding: generic image/headshot generation, generic "chat with PDF/website" bots, boilerplates and prompt packs. Evidence is indirect (flat leader revenue, many tiny clones, ShipFast decline).
- Better odds: B2B tools tied to a workflow and a system of record, where the buyer pays monthly for an outcome; Stripe's B2B/B2C gap supports this.
- Selling to indie founders works for a few people with large audiences; it is audience-dependent rather than a repeatable niche.

### Gaps
- No revenue data found for add-ins/extensions specifically (Excel, Power BI, Shopify, WordPress, browser extensions). I ran no dedicated search on app-store ecosystems; this is unanswered rather than negative.
- No data found on SEO-driven AI utilities as a category in 2026.

## 4. What role do vibe-coding tools play, and do apps built with them earn real revenue?

### Takeaway
The verified money is in the tools, not yet in the apps built with them: Lovable reports $500M annualised revenue, while evidence that vibe-coded apps earn meaningful revenue is self-reported and has no distribution. Individual cases exist, but several are short-lived.

### Cited Findings
- Lovable said it passed $500M annualised revenue in June 2026 (from $400M in Feb/Mar 2026, with 146 employees), computed as last month's revenue times 12; not confirmed by filings — [TechCrunch, 9 Jun 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/09/lovable-says-it-has-hit-500m-in-annualized-revenue-with-1-million-new-projects-a-week/); [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/lovable-build-economy-500m-arr-vibe-coding).
- Sacra estimates Lovable at $600M annualised by September 2026 — [Sacra](https://www.sacra.com/c/lovable).
- A 2026 Lovable report claims over 50 million apps created and about 35% "generating revenue", without a distribution of outcomes; survey skews to enthusiasts — [summary page](https://goodfest.westfield.com/2026/06/5-surprising-things-we-just-learned-about-the-people-who-use-lovable/) (low-quality secondary source; treat as unverified).
- Base44, itself a vibe-coding tool built by a tiny team, sold for $80M six months after founding — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/18/6-month-old-solo-owned-vibe-coder-base44-sells-to-wix-for-80m-cash).
- Levels' Cursor-built flight simulator reached a $1M/year run rate in 17 days and then fell to zero — [dev.to](https://dev.to/promptway/he-built-a-flight-simulator-in-three-hours-and-hit-1m-a-year-in-17-days-then-it-went-to-zero-1b1l).
- Stripe Atlas: share of startups charging a first customer within 30 days rose to 20% in 2025 from 8% in 2020 — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/stripe-atlas-startups-in-2025-year-in-review). One secondary source says the 2026 Atlas cohort earns 5x the 2025 class at the same point; unconfirmed — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/stripe-atlas-130-percent-startup-incorporations-q1-2026).
- Coverage notes that Lovable and the projects on it are too young to show durability — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/09/lovable-says-it-has-hit-500m-in-annualized-revenue-with-1-million-new-projects-a-week/).

### Inferences
- Vibe coding lowers build cost and time to first charge, which raises supply; Stripe's falling median with a rising top decile is consistent with more entrants competing for the same demand.
- "35% generating revenue" says nothing about amounts; a single $5 sale would count.
- Distribution, not building, is the bottleneck: the vibe-coded successes found belong to founders with large existing audiences.

### Gaps
- No verified dataset linking build tool (Lovable, Bolt, Cursor, Claude Code, Replit) to app revenue.
- No figures found for Bolt or Replit-built app revenue.

## 5. Gross margins, churn, model cost share and risks

### Takeaway
AI products run at roughly 45–60% gross margin versus 70–90% for classic SaaS, with inference near a quarter of revenue, and consumer AI apps churn about 30% faster. Platform absorption is documented in specific cases, but the "90% of wrappers will die" statistics are unsourced.

### Cited Findings
- ICONIQ State of AI (as relayed by secondary sources): average AI product gross margin ~45% in 2025, projected 53% in 2026 and 59% in 2027 — [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/ai-gross-margin); [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/have-ai-gross-margins-really-turned-the-corner/).
- Bessemer puts AI-first companies at roughly 50–60% gross margin versus 70–90% for mature SaaS — [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/ai-gross-margin).
- ICONIQ January 2026: inference is about 23% of revenue at scaling-stage AI B2B companies and does not meaningfully fall with scale — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/ai-startup-growth/ai-inference-costs-gross-margin) (secondary).
- Counter-example at the solo end: Photo AI reported ~$80k profit on ~$105k revenue (about 76% profit margin) in March 2026 — [andrew.ooo](https://andrew.ooo/posts/pieter-levels-photo-ai-solo-founder/) (self-claimed).
- RevenueCat 2026: AI apps retain 21.1% of annual subscribers at 12 months (non-AI 30.7%) and 6.1% of monthly subscribers (non-AI 9.5%) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows).
- RevenueCat study of 3,519 AI apps: high-retention AI apps keep a median 13.9% of paid subscriptions after a year; low-retention apps keep 1.4% — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ai-app-retention-study).
- B2B contrast: Chatbase reported 92% retention at $8M ARR — [ProductLed](https://productled.com/blog/how-chatbase-hit-8m-arr-with-18-people).
- TechCrunch's "AI graveyard" (updated 14 Nov 2026) lists platform-driven failures, e.g. Rabbit R1 shut down May 2026 as larger platforms offered similar experiences; OpenAI folded stand-alone apps into ChatGPT — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/the-ai-graveyard-a-running-list-of-projects-and-startups-that-didnt-make-it/).
- TechRadar analysis: of US AI startups that wound down in 2025, 81% were "wrappers and apps" (underlying dataset not verified) — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/when-the-model-takes-away-the-moat).
- The "90% of AI wrappers face closure in 2026" claim has no cited methodology; opinion, not data — [dev.to](https://dev.to/autonainews/90-of-ai-wrapper-startups-face-2026-closures-as-value-add-strategy-crumbles-7d4).

### Inferences
- Margin depends on product type more than on size: image generation with per-credit pricing can be very profitable solo; heavy LLM usage on flat subscriptions is where margins collapse.
- Consumer AI churn means revenue has to be re-bought through marketing every year; small B2B products keep customers far longer.
- Other risks implied by the cases: dependence on one channel (X audience, app-store ranking, Google), on one model supplier's pricing, and revenue reporting that mixes annual prepayments with MRR.

### Gaps
- No margin, churn or API-cost benchmarks specific to solo/micro-SaaS AI products; the ICONIQ/Bessemer figures cover funded companies and were read only through secondary summaries.
- No reliable closure rate for AI wrappers.

## 6. AI services / agency model versus product

### Takeaway
No reliable data exists on what AI automation agencies earn; nearly all figures come from agencies, course sellers and tool vendors. The grounded picture is project fees of $2k–$12k, early revenue dominated by one-off setup fees, and $10k/month being reachable with 3–5 retainer clients.

### Cited Findings
- Typical custom build price for one client: $2,000–$12,000, no recurring revenue unless a retainer is added — [CustomGPT](https://customgpt.ai/make-money-with-ai-agents-income-tiers/) (vendor blog).
- "Realistic path to $10K MRR is 3–5 retainer clients on the same agent template" — [CustomGPT](https://customgpt.ai/make-money-with-ai-agents-income-tiers/).
- Early-stage agency revenue is about 75% one-time setup fees — [Ciela](https://ciela.ai/blogs/how-much-money-can-you-make-ai-automation-agency) (agency-tool vendor, Mar 2026).
- The most-upvoted Reddit threads often expose "$30k/month" agency claims as course revenue presented as agency revenue — [Ciela, 2 Jul 2026](https://ciela.ai/blogs/ai-automation-agency-realistic-income-reddit).
- "AI automation agencies charge $5k a client; few get five" — [Medium](https://medium.com/@automation.labs/ai-automation-agencies-charge-5k-a-client-few-get-five-f0e9ad0b5dde) (title only; opinion piece).
- Aggressive claims of $10k–$50k/month within 6–12 months, or $50k+ MRR in 12 months, appear on promotional sites with no sample shown — [agentincome blog](https://agentincome-blog.pages.dev/blog/ai-automation-agency-2026).

### Inferences
- Services give faster first revenue and lower platform risk than a product, but income is capped by hours and sales capacity; for a domain expert (for example finance/BI) a productised service on one repeatable template is the lower-variance route.
- Much of the visible "agency income" is education revenue, the same pattern as tools-for-founders in question 3.

### Gaps
- No survey, tax or marketplace data on agency or consultant income; no failure rate. Upwork/Fiverr rate data was not searched.
- Reddit threads were not read directly; their content is relayed by a vendor blog.
