# Consumer AI apps and AI creative/prosumer tools: who makes real money (state: November 2026)

Method note for the report writer: all figures below come from web-search result summaries gathered on 2026-11-25. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (a16z.com, techcrunch.com and sensortower.com were outside the allowed network domains), so **no source page was opened and read in full**. Figures are therefore as rendered in search snippets and should be treated as one step removed from the primary source. Labels used: **VERIFIED** = audited/regulatory filing (SEC, statutory accounts); **REPORTED** = third-party estimate or press report (Sensor Tower, Appfigures, Sacra, The Information, Latka); **SELF-CLAIMED** = company or founder statement, unaudited.

## 1. Which consumer AI apps earn the most in 2025–2026 (Sensor Tower, Appfigures, a16z, RevenueCat)?

### Takeaway
Consumer AI app-store spend is real and large but extremely concentrated: generative-AI in-app purchase revenue topped roughly $5B in 2025 (more than tripling) and was still growing about 103% year over year in Q3 2026, with ChatGPT alone accounting for about $3.4B in 2025 and becoming the #1 grossing app worldwide in Q3 2026. Outside the labs, the largest earners are creative platforms that bill mostly on the web (Canva, Higgsfield, ElevenLabs, Midjourney, Suno, Runway, HeyGen), not app-store-native apps.

### Cited Findings
- Q3 2026: ChatGPT became the #1 grossing app worldwide for the first time, overtaking TikTok; global IAP revenue for the quarter was $45B (+4.4% YoY); in-app spending on generative AI apps grew 103% YoY. REPORTED (Sensor Tower estimate, via secondary coverage) — [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-chatgpt-tops-tiktok-as-no-1-grossing-app-in-a-45b-quarter/); primary report at [Sensor Tower Q3 2026 Digital Market Index](https://sensortower.com/blog/q3-2026-digital-market-index) (not opened)
- Q2 2026: top IAP revenue apps were TikTok, ChatGPT and Google One; non-gaming apps drove spending growth while games fell 4.5%. REPORTED — [9to5Mac, 2026-08-11](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/11/report-non-gaming-apps-drove-mobile-spending-growth-in-q2-as-games-fell-4-5/)
- Full-year 2025: gen-AI IAP revenue more than tripled to over $5B; downloads doubled to over 3.8B; consumers spent more in apps than in games for the first time. REPORTED (Sensor Tower State of Mobile 2026) — [Sensor Tower press release, 2026-01-21](https://sensortower.com/press/press-release-boosted-by-gen-ai-services-consumers-spent-more-money-in-apps-than-games-for-first-time); [TechCrunch, 2026-01-21](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/21/consumers-spent-more-on-mobile-apps-than-games-in-2025-driven-by-ai-app-adoption); [The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/state-of-mobile-2026-report-shows-ai-chatbot-search-and-assistant-growth-outpaces-gaming-social-and-more)

### Inferences
- App-store data understates the non-lab winners: Sensor Tower/Appfigures only see IAP, while Midjourney, ElevenLabs, Suno, Runway, HeyGen, Higgsfield and Gamma bill mainly through the web. A ranking of "who earns most" must combine IAP estimates with ARR disclosures.
- With ChatGPT at about $3.4B of a roughly $5B+ category in 2025, all other AI apps together shared well under $2B of app-store spend; the long tail is thin.
- The a16z card-spend data (top 1% at $903/month) suggests consumer AI revenue is increasingly driven by a small group of heavy "prosumer" payers stacking several subscriptions, not by broad mass-market conversion.

### Gaps
- The full a16z 7th-edition ranking (top 50 web / top 50 mobile) and its per-product payer data could not be retrieved; a16z.com was blocked.
- No named Sensor Tower or Appfigures 2026 ranking of non-assistant AI apps by revenue was retrieved (the Q3 2026 Digital Market Index was blocked).
- No data.ai-branded 2026 figures were found (data.ai is part of Sensor Tower; its State of Mobile is the successor).
- The Q3 2026 figures come from secondary coverage (FourWeekMBA), not the Sensor Tower page itself.

## 4. Retention and churn of consumer AI subscriptions versus non-AI apps; durable versus fad

### Takeaway
RevenueCat's 2026 data shows AI apps monetize faster but keep customers worse: better trial conversion and higher revenue per user, but annual-plan retention of 21.1% versus 30.7% for non-AI apps and monthly 12-month retention of 6.1% versus 9.5%. Durable revenue sits with workflow/creator tools and habit products; novelty photo and companion apps look fad-like.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 covers over 115,000 apps and more than $16B in revenue. — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)
- Annual retention (after 12 months): 21.1% for AI apps versus 30.7% for non-AI apps; annual churn about 30% faster for AI apps at the median. — [TechCrunch, 2026-03-10](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)
- Monthly plans, 12-month retention: 6.1% for AI apps versus 9.5% for non-AI apps. — [TechCrunch, 2026-03-10](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)
- Weekly plans: TechCrunch reports AI apps retain better (2.5% versus 1.7%); a separate summary gives 1.2% versus 1.7%. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows); contradicted by [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)
- AI apps earn 41% more revenue per user; trial-to-paid conversion is 52% better (8.5% versus 5.6% at the median); refund rates about 20% higher. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)
- A secondary write-up frames it as AI apps losing 79% of annual subscribers before month 13. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/zaraai_0b75675ddc9204c716/ai-apps-generate-41-more-revenue-per-user-but-lose-79-of-annual-subscribers-before-month-13-485a)
- RevenueCat follow-up study of 3,519 AI apps: high-retention apps keep 13.9% of paid subscriptions after a year at the median; low-retention apps keep 1.4%. — [RevenueCat AI app retention study](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ai-app-retention-study)
- Durability signals by product: Remini revenue declining in Q2 2026 — [ARR Club](https://www.arr.club/bending-spoons/bending-spoons-hit-1-3b-arr-with-accelerated-growth-driven-by-ai); Replika store revenue down to roughly $400K/month — [AI Companion Pick](https://aicompanionpick.com/replika-user-base-2026); Duolingo paid subscribers still growing 14% YoY — [Duolingo 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069692/duol-20260930.htm)

### Inferences
- The tenfold spread between high- and low-retention AI apps (13.9% versus 1.4%) matters more than the AI/non-AI average: "AI app" is not one category.
- Likely durable: tools embedded in recurring work or habit (design, video/voice production for creators and marketers, language learning). Likely fad-prone: one-shot novelty generation (avatars, photo enhancers) and companions with weak willingness to pay. This is an inference from the limited product data above, not a RevenueCat finding.
- Higher conversion plus higher refunds and faster churn is consistent with curiosity-driven purchases: users pay to try the output, then leave.

### Gaps
- RevenueCat's per-category AI retention breakdown (e.g. photo/video versus productivity versus education) was not retrieved.
- The weekly-retention figure is contradictory between sources and could not be checked against the report.
- No retention or churn disclosures were found for individual companies (Suno, Midjourney, ElevenLabs, etc.).

## 5. Pricing models and acquisition channels of the successful ones

### Takeaway
App-store-native AI apps lean on weekly subscriptions and hard paywalls to cover inference cost and capture impulse demand, while the larger web-first creative tools use monthly/annual tiers plus credits. Distribution is dominated by paid ads; influencer/UGC is decisive for a few breakouts (Cal AI) but evidence on it is mostly anecdotal or vendor-sourced.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat: AI apps need a different growth model because every user has a serving cost; many skip freemium and use a hard paywall to limit GPU cost from non-payers; the most engaged trial users are also the most expensive to serve. — [RevenueCat: AI subscription app pricing](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ai-subscription-app-pricing/)
- Weekly plans are gaining ground in AI-generation and "churn-and-burn" utility apps even though annual plans have the highest LTV (vendor blog, directional). — [Stormy AI](https://stormy.ai/blog/monetizing-ai-influencer-apps-social-proof-custom-prompts)
- Adapty: paid ads still lead subscription-app growth; even apps with dedicated influencer teams get most installs from paid; in one app's analytics 97% of acquisition came from paid ads and 3% from creators (single-app example, vendor source). — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/creators-vs-paid-ads-for-subscription-apps/)
- Cal AI generated more than $30M in two years "almost entirely through paid influencer marketing", per its founder (SELF-CLAIMED). — [ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/cal-ai-30m-revenue-myfitnesspal-acquisition-2026)
- Canva reports rising LLM referral traffic as an acquisition channel. — [TechCrunch, 2026-02-18](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/18/canva-gets-to-4b-in-revenue-as-llm-referral-traffic-rises/)
- Price points: Character.AI c.ai+ $9.99/month — [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/ai-map/company/character-ai); Replika Pro $19.99/month or $69.99/year — [AI Companion Pick](https://aicompanionpick.com/replika-user-base-2026); assistants' top tiers at about $100/month, with Claude having 7.3% of payers on Max — [a16z 7th edition](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-7/)
- Suno's February 2026 figures ($300M ARR, 2M paid subscribers) imply about $150 ARR per subscriber, i.e. about $12.50/month (calculation from self-claimed figures). — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-hits-2m-paid-subscribers-300m-annual-revenue/)
- Duolingo folded premium AI features into its standard Super tier as AI costs fell. — [Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/415042/duolingos-user-surge-clears-the-whisper-but-paid-subscribers-and-q4-profits-still-trail-the-dau-story)
- Gamma reached $100M ARR profitably with about 50 people. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/gamma-grant-lee-profitable-ai-startup)

### Inferences
- Two playbooks coexist: (a) mobile "wrapper" apps with hard paywall, weekly pricing and paid/influencer acquisition, which produce fast revenue but low retention; (b) web-first prosumer tools with monthly/annual plans and credit metering, organic/community and SEO-led growth, small teams and higher durability (Midjourney, Gamma, Suno).
- Heavy users are increasingly monetized via high tiers ($100+/month) and credits, which fits the a16z finding that the top 1% spend $903/month.
- Falling inference cost pushes AI features down into base tiers (Duolingo), so premium AI tiers may be a transitional pricing device.

### Gaps
- No reliable quantitative data found on the share of AI apps using weekly versus monthly versus annual plans, or on credit-based pricing prevalence.
- Evidence on TikTok/UGC and SEO as channels is anecdotal or vendor marketing; no independent benchmark was retrieved (an Airbridge 2026 UGC handbook exists but its figures were not visible).
- Current price lists for Midjourney, ElevenLabs, Runway, HeyGen, Higgsfield and Suno were not collected in this pass.
