# AI application-layer market and revenue leaders (state as of November 2026)

> **Method note for the report writer (read first).** Research date: 2026-11-25. All findings below come from web-search result summaries. Direct page fetches failed in this session for every host tried (menlovc.com, sec.gov, aibusiness.vc, clinkbill.com were not reachable from the sandbox), so **no primary document was opened and read in full**. Figures are therefore as relayed by search snippets; exact wording, dates and definitions should be re-checked against the linked source before publication. Where a figure could only be traced to an aggregator/blog, that is stated.
>
> **Evidence labels used throughout:**
> - **VERIFIED** = audited filing / SEC document / official earnings release of a public company.
> - **REPORTED** = press reporting or analyst estimate based on unnamed sources / investor material (The Information, Bloomberg, TechCrunch, Sacra estimates, Dealroom).
> - **SELF-CLAIMED** = company or founder statement (blog, X post, press release of a private company), unaudited.
> - **AGGREGATOR** = low-authority secondary site; treat as a pointer only.
>
> "ARR", "annualized revenue" and "run-rate" are used loosely and interchangeably in the press; almost none of the private-company figures are contractual ARR or recognized GAAP revenue.

## 1. How large is the AI application / generative AI software market in 2025 and 2026, and how is spend split between applications, models and infrastructure?

### Takeaway
Menlo Ventures (the most-cited bottom-up source) puts enterprise AI application spend at about $19B in 2025 and about $77B in 2026, inside total enterprise AI deployment spend of $176.8B (up 5.4x from $33B); Gartner's far broader top-down definition gives "AI software" of about $288B (2025) and $462B (2026) within $2.7T of total AI spending. The two are not comparable, and in Menlo's 2026 data the three frontier labs capture 69% of deployment spending, so the independent application layer is a minority of the money.

### Inferences
- Menlo changed scope between editions: the 2025 report's headline total was $37B "generative AI spend", while the 2026 report restates the prior-year base as $33B "enterprise AI deployment spending". The application figure ($19B) carries over unchanged, so application share was about 51-58% in 2025 and about 44% ($77B of $176.8B) in 2026: applications grew about 4x but lost share to models/API/infrastructure, which grew faster.
- If labs take 69% ($122.7B) of the $176.8B and applications are $77B, a large part of "application" spend must be first-party lab products (ChatGPT Enterprise, Claude/Claude Code, Gemini). The independent, non-lab application layer is therefore materially smaller than $77B; the exact figure was not available.
- Coding alone ($34.0B) is about 44% of Menlo's 2026 application spend, and grew about 8.5x from $4.0B. The application layer in 2026 is to a large extent a coding-tools market.
- Gartner's "AI software" ($462B) is roughly 6x Menlo's application number because it counts AI embedded in all software sold by incumbents, worldwide. Use Menlo for "new AI product revenue", Gartner for "software spend touched by AI".

### Gaps
- The Menlo 2026 PDF could not be opened (host blocked), so the full layer split (applications vs. models vs. infrastructure in dollars), the departmental/vertical/horizontal split and category amounts other than coding are missing for 2026. menlovc.com needs to be allowed in the session's network settings to close this.
- No IDC, Sequoia or a16z market-size figure for the 2026 application layer was found.
- No reliable consumer-side (non-enterprise) application market total was found; Menlo's numbers are enterprise-only.
- Reconciliation of the $12.6B "moved to AI-native vendors" figure with the $77B application total is unclear (secondary source only).

## 3. Which categories capture the most spend, and which grow fastest?

### Takeaway
Coding is by a wide margin both the largest and the fastest-growing application category: Menlo puts it at $34.0B in 2026 versus $4.0B in 2025 (about 8.5x), with 96% going to AI-native vendors. Outside coding, startups lead in customer support, creative media and vertical niches (legal, healthcare), while incumbents hold IT, data science and HR.

## 5. Which companies are actually profitable or cash-flow positive versus burning cash?

### Takeaway
Confirmed profitability is rare and concentrated in bootstrapped or capital-light consumer/creative tools (Midjourney; Canva marginally; HeyGen about break-even; ElevenLabs reportedly) and in public incumbents. The fastest-growing coding apps resell model inference at thin margins: Cursor's gross margin was described as only recently slightly positive, and average AI product gross margins are about 45-53% versus 70-80% for classic SaaS.

### Cited Findings
- Midjourney: profitable with zero outside funding; reportedly profitable about six months after launch (founder statement; Forbes listing). **REPORTED / SELF-CLAIMED**, no audited accounts. — [SQ Magazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/how-many-people-work-at-midjourney/); [Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/midjourney-shows-the-way-to-success-without-vc-funding/)
- Canva: 2025 Australian statutory group accounts reportedly show $3.02B revenue and $25.96M profit after tax (under 1% net margin). **AGGREGATOR relaying statutory accounts**; unverified. Canva also cut its 2026 growth forecast from 30% to 20% because AI features cost more to serve than planned. **REPORTED**. — [ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/how-does-canva-make-money-freemium-subscriptions-enterprise-and-valuation); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/canva-cuts-revenue-forecast-ai-costs/)
- ElevenLabs: listed as profitable with an "estimated $116M 2025 profit". **AGGREGATOR, single source**. — [Newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-infrastructure-profitable-startups)
- HeyGen: around cash-flow break-even with only about $25M cumulative burn. **AGGREGATOR**. — [Newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-infrastructure-growing-without-burning-billions)
- Mercor: reportedly generating positive free cash flow. **REPORTED via aggregator**. — [Newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-infrastructure-growing-without-burning-billions)
- Gamma: $100M ARR on $91M total raised with about 50 employees (capital efficiency; profitability itself not confirmed here). **REPORTED**. — [Clinkbill](https://clinkbill.com/arr-leaderboard)
- Cursor: gross margin "only recently turned slightly positive". **AGGREGATOR**. — [aibusiness.vc revenue leaderboard](https://aibusiness.vc/startups/ai-revenue-leaderboard)
- Industry margins: AI product gross margin 45% (2025), 53% expected (2026), 59% (2027) per ICONIQ survey; another estimate 41% (2024) to 52% (2026), with inference/hosting costs consuming 40-50% of revenue. — [ICONIQ](https://www.iconiq.com/growth/insights/state-of-ai-2026); [Offset Partners](https://www.offsetpartners.io/resources/ai-startup-gross-margin-benchmarks)
- Context (labs, not applications): Anthropic's Q3 2026 operating margin was reported at -3% (adjusted +15%); Databricks reports positive adjusted free cash flow over the last twelve months (company press release). — [aibusiness.vc](https://aibusiness.vc/startups/ai-revenue-leaderboard)
- Public incumbents (Microsoft, Adobe, Salesforce, ServiceNow) are profitable at group level, but do not disclose margins on their AI products; Adobe posted record Q3 FY2026 revenue of $6.76B. **VERIFIED** for group results. — [Adobe press release](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260910832552/adobe-reports-record-q3-results)

### Inferences
- Rough classification from available evidence:
  - Profitable / cash-generating: Midjourney (reported), Canva (thin, reportedly), ElevenLabs (single-source), Mercor (reported), incumbents at group level.
  - About break-even or highly capital-efficient: HeyGen, Gamma.
  - Burning or thin-margin while hyper-growing: Cursor (gross margin near zero until recently), and by implication Lovable, Replit and Cognition, which resell third-party model inference; no direct burn figures were found for these.
  - Unknown: Harvey, Glean, Sierra, Decagon, Perplexity, Suno, Abridge, OpenEvidence, Clay, Synthesia.
- The pattern suggests profitability correlates with owning the model or having low inference cost per dollar of revenue (Midjourney, ElevenLabs train their own models) and with consumer subscription pricing, whereas apps built on frontier-lab APIs (coding tools) pass a large share of revenue through to the labs. This fits Menlo's finding that 69% of deployment spend ends up at three labs.
- Canva's forecast cut is a concrete data point that AI features can compress margins and growth even at a scaled, profitable incumbent-like company.

### Gaps
- No audited or reliably reported net income / free cash flow / burn figures were found for any AI-native startup in the ranking; all profitability statements are self-claimed or aggregator-relayed.
- Gross margin data for Lovable, Replit, Cognition, Perplexity, Harvey, Sierra: not found.
- The Information / Bloomberg reporting on burn rates (e.g. Cursor, Perplexity) is paywalled and was not retrieved.
- Hosts that must be allowed to verify the primary documents: menlovc.com, www.sec.gov, aibusiness.vc, clinkbill.com.
