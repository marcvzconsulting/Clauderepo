# B2B and vertical AI products: who makes real money (state as of November 2026)

Legend for revenue labels used throughout:
- **VERIFIED** = public-company filing/earnings release (audited or regulated disclosure).
- **SELF-CLAIMED** = company press release, blog or CEO statement (unaudited).
- **REPORTED** = journalist reporting from sources/documents (The Information, TechCrunch, Fortune, STAT).
- **ESTIMATE** = third-party analyst estimate (Sacra, Dealroom, aggregators). Not a disclosure.

Method caveats: almost all figures below come from search-result summaries of the linked pages; full-page fetches of saastr.com and chartmogul.com were blocked by the session network policy, so details from those two were not read in full. "ARR" for AI companies is frequently annualised run-rate (last month x 12) including usage/consumption revenue, not contracted subscription ARR. No private-company figure below is audited.

## 1. Per category: who are the leaders and what is their revenue and growth?

### Takeaway
Coding is by far the largest B2B AI application category (multiple products at $0.5B-$4B+ run-rate), followed by customer support, healthcare, legal, voice and enterprise search, each of which has one to three companies at $100M-$600M; finance, meeting notes, AI SDR, construction, insurance and logistics have leaders at well under $100M or no disclosed revenue. Only the incumbents' numbers (Salesforce, ServiceNow, Microsoft) are verified; nearly every start-up figure is self-claimed or a Sacra estimate.

## 2. Which categories show the strongest willingness to pay and best retention, and which show high churn or "experimental budget" problems?

### Takeaway
Evidence of durable revenue is strongest where AI is sold to enterprises on contracts with expansion (coding, support agents, legal, enterprise search); churn is worst in low-priced, self-serve AI tools and in AI SDR/outbound and generic copywriting. Hard retention metrics for individual leaders are almost never disclosed, so most of this rests on aggregate studies and indirect signals.

### Cited Findings
- ChartMogul study of ~3,500 software companies (2025 data): AI-native companies had median gross revenue retention of ~40% and net revenue retention of ~48% — worse than B2C software; AI-native products under $50/month had ~23% GRR; retention is substantially better for those that reach $5M ARR — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/); [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/the-ai-churn-wave)
- a16z (Sept 2025) counter-argument: leading AI companies have a measurement problem rather than a retention problem; "AI tourists" churn in months 0-3, so retention should be benchmarked from month 3 — [a16z](https://a16z.com/ai-retention-benchmarks/)
- Gainsight podcast/newsletter theme: $100M-ARR AI companies are struggling at renewals (practitioner opinion) — [Gainsight Unchurned](https://unchurned.gainsight.com/p/the-100m-arr-problem-no-one-wants)
- Expansion evidence, support: ~50% of Agentforce + Data 360 bookings came from existing-customer expansion (Q3 FY26); in Q2 FY27 about half of Agentforce bookings came from customers replenishing usage credits; accounts with agents in production +70% q/q (VERIFIED) — [Salesforce FY26 Q3](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2025/12/03/fy26-q3-earnings/); [Nasdaq call summary](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/salesforce-q2-earnings-call-highlights)
- Expansion evidence, coding: ~75% of Cursor's run-rate reportedly comes from enterprise customers; GitHub Copilot revenue +60% q/q after moving to usage pricing — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/134107-cursor-tops-4b-annualized-revenue/); [Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/07/microsoft-msft-q4-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Churn evidence, AI SDR: 11x ex-employees alleged 70-80% churn; company claimed 79% retention; customers exited via 3-month break clauses (2025) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have)
- Churn/stagnation evidence, generic content: Jasper cut its internal valuation as growth slowed after ChatGPT competition — [The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/jasper-an-early-generative-ai-winner-cuts-internal-valuation-as-growth-slows)
- Meeting notes: Otter spend plateaued and Fathom/Fireflies spend declined while Granola gained (switching, not category growth) — [YipitData](https://www.yipitdata.com/resources/blog/granola-vs-fathom-otter-fireflies-ai-notetaking)
- Glean passed $300M with "AI budget cutting" (consolidating overlapping AI tools) as a selling point, implying buyers are now pruning experimental tools — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/gleans-top-line-crosses-300m-as-ai-budget-cutting-becomes-its-major-selling-point/)
- a16z CIO survey (prior-year edition, via SaaStr): innovation-budget share of enterprise AI spend fell from 25% to 7%, i.e. spend moved into permanent budget lines (secondary source; 2025 survey) — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/a16z-enterprise-ai-spending-is-growing-75-a-year/)
- Bessemer State of AI 2025: fast-growing "Supernova" AI start-ups average ~25% gross margin (often negative) with fragile retention; "Shooting Stars" grow slower at ~60% gross margin — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-state-of-ai-2025)

### Inferences
- Strongest willingness to pay: coding (per-developer and usage spend that keeps expanding), support agents (direct labour substitution with measurable resolutions), legal (high-billing professionals, firm-wide contracts), clinical documentation in health systems (enterprise contracts, though price pressure from bundling).
- Weakest/most "experimental": AI SDR/outbound, generic marketing copy, low-priced notetakers and other sub-$50/month self-serve tools — consistent with the 23% GRR ChartMogul bucket.
- Usage-credit "replenishment" at Salesforce is real consumption but is less predictable than seat renewals; high run-rate does not equal contracted recurring revenue.

### Gaps
- No NRR/GRR disclosed by Sierra, Decagon, Harvey, Legora, Abridge, Glean, Cursor, Clay or ElevenLabs in the sources found. Unsourced consultant pages quoting NRR (e.g. Jasper 115%, Writer 126%) were excluded as unverifiable.
- No 2026 update of the ChartMogul study was found; the 40%/48% figures are 2025 data and could not be read in full (host blocked).

## 3. What pricing models work and what are typical price points?

### Takeaway
Support has converged on outcome/usage pricing at roughly $1-2 per resolution or conversation; coding and incumbents are shifting from seats to usage/credits; legal, scribes and search remain mostly per-seat enterprise contracts. Contract-size data is scarce.

### Cited Findings
- Fin: $0.99 per resolution list price, charged only on successful resolutions — [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/learning-center/fin-vs-zendesk)
- Zendesk AI: per "verified/automated resolution", roughly $1.50 committed / $2.00 pay-as-you-go according to Intercom's comparison; a third party gives $1.20-$2.00; Zendesk says rates are negotiated and not published — [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/learning-center/fin-vs-zendesk); [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/intercom-fin-vs-zendesk-ai-vs-agentforce-2026/); [Zendesk](https://www.zendesk.com/service/comparison/zendesk-vs-fin/)
- Salesforce Agentforce: $2.00 per conversation regardless of resolution, on top of Service Cloud Enterprise ($175/user/month); consumption via usage credits ("work units": 3.2B in Q2 FY27, +97% q/q) — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/intercom-fin-vs-zendesk-ai-vs-agentforce-2026/); [Salesforce FY27 Q2](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/08/26/fy27-q2-earnings/)
- Intercom added usage-based AI pricing alongside seats — [Sacra chat summary](https://sacra.com/chat/h/a419a844-b238-4e9d-94c0-bae80585618f/)
- GitHub Copilot moved to usage-based pricing in June 2026, after which revenue accelerated >60% q/q — [Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/07/microsoft-msft-q4-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Counter-trend: Atlassian and HubSpot reported as joining a shift to AI flat fees (headline only; paywalled) — [The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/atlassian-hubspot-join-shift-ai-flat-fees?rc=0cqymi)
- Clay sells credits (usage) on top of plans; critics say this makes its "ARR" less recurring — [LeadGenius](https://www.leadgenius.com/resources/the-clay-bubble-why-100m-arr-isnt-what-it-seems-and-why-ai-might-be-the-pin-that-pops-it)
- OpenEvidence is free to clinicians, monetised by pharma/device advertising — [Sacra, OpenEvidence](https://sacra.com/c/openevidence/)
- Scribes: Nabla ~$119 per clinician per month (low end) — [Sacra, Abridge vs Ambience vs Freed](https://sacra.com/research/abridge-vs-ambience-vs-freed/)
- Glean: partly consumption-priced, so the headline number is partly run-rate — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/gleans-top-line-crosses-300m-as-ai-budget-cutting-becomes-its-major-selling-point/)
- Harvey pricing reference page exists (per-seat enterprise; details not read) — [Metronome pricing index](https://metronome.com/pricing-index/harvey)

### Inferences
- Outcome pricing works where the outcome is countable and attributable (ticket resolved); it has not spread to legal, finance or healthcare, where seats or enterprise licences dominate.
- Usage pricing raises revenue for vendors with heavy users (Copilot, Cursor, Agentforce credits) but makes "ARR" less comparable and less predictable.
- AI SDR vendors' use of short break clauses (3 months) is associated with the highest observed churn.

### Gaps

- No sourced data on what share of the market uses seat vs usage vs outcome pricing in 2026; the Menlo report may contain this but the PDF was not read.

## 4. Which B2B categories are crowded/commoditised versus under-served?

### Takeaway
Crowded or commoditising: coding assistants, meeting notetakers, AI scribes, AI SDR/outbound, generic content generation, and increasingly support agents (incumbent bundling). Under-served by revenue leaders: construction, insurance, logistics, back-office document processing, accounting — categories where I found funding but no disclosed revenue at scale.

### Cited Findings
- Menlo Ventures 2026 (published 3 Nov 2026): enterprise AI application spend ~$77B (from $19B); coding $34B and assistants/productivity $25B are 76% of it; vertical AI only $10.1B, led by healthcare $3.7B and legal $1.8B (Menlo estimate; Menlo is an Anthropic investor) — [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2026/11/03/menlo-venturest-enterprise-ai-spending-hits-177b-up-5-4x-in-one-year-as-agents-take-over/); [Menlo report PDF](https://menlovc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/menlo_ventures_enterprise_ai_report-2026-110326.pdf)
- AI-natives hold 77% of application spend (up from 63%); Anthropic, OpenAI and Google capture ~69% of total enterprise AI spend ($122.7B of $176.8B) — [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2026/11/03/menlo-venturest-enterprise-ai-spending-hits-177b-up-5-4x-in-one-year-as-agents-take-over/)
- Only ~$12.6B of spend moved from SaaS incumbents to AI-natives; only ~22% of AI spend came out of software budgets (secondary summaries of Menlo) — [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-grew-5-4x-but-only-12-6b-moved-from-saas-to-ai-natives/); [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/ai-spend-hit-177-billion-this-year-only-22-of-it-came-out-of-software-budgets/)
- Scribes: Sacra calls the standalone scribe "effectively dead", bundled free into other products; Epic/Microsoft competition is a recurring theme — [Sacra, Freed vs Heidi Health](https://sacra.com/research/freed-vs-heidi-health/); [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/02/10/abridge-ai-scribe-cto-talks-epic-microsoft-rebranding/)
- Finance research tools (Rogo, Hebbia) face a direct threat from model labs' own finance products — [The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/newsletters/ai-agenda/rogo-hebbia-revenue-rises-claude-finance-threat-looms)
- Support: Decagon "reportedly exploring a sale"; Fin sold to Salesforce at ~9x ARR — consolidation signals — [Sacra, Decagon](https://sacra.com/c/decagon/); [Salesforce press release](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/06/15/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-fin)

### Inferences
- Vertical AI is ~13% of application spend ($10.1B of ~$77B): most money still flows to horizontal coding and assistants, where three model labs and a handful of products dominate. Verticals outside healthcare and legal total only ~$4.6B across all industries, i.e. they are either under-served or simply slow to buy.
- Categories where the model labs or suite vendors can bundle the feature (notes, scribes, copy, search, finance research) show the clearest commoditisation risk; categories with proprietary workflow/data integration (personal-injury demand letters, clinical ads network, GTM data enrichment) retain pricing power.
- "Under-served" in construction/insurance/logistics is an inference from the absence of disclosed revenue, not from positive evidence of unmet demand.

## 5. What evidence exists on enterprise ROI from these products?

### Takeaway
The evidence is mixed: spend is exploding and moving into permanent budgets (Menlo, a16z), but independent studies (MIT NANDA) and retention data (ChartMogul) show most pilots do not yield measurable P&L impact and many AI products churn heavily; bought vertical tools succeed more often than internal builds.

### Cited Findings
- MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (Aug 2025): ~95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact; ~5% achieved rapid revenue acceleration. Method: 150 leader interviews, 350-employee survey, 300 public deployments. Purchased/partnered tools succeeded about two-thirds of the time versus one-third for internal builds. Failure attributed to integration/learning gaps, not model quality. The headline number is contested as a small-sample directional finding — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)
- Menlo Ventures 2026: enterprise AI spend $176.8B as of Sept 2026, up 5.4x from $33B; applications ~$77B — [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2026/11/03/menlo-venturest-enterprise-ai-spending-hits-177b-up-5-4x-in-one-year-as-agents-take-over/)
- a16z third annual CIO survey (Jan 2026; 100 Global-2000 companies): average enterprise LLM spend rose from ~$2.5M (2024) to ~$7M (2025), expected ~$11.6M in 2026 — [a16z](https://a16z.com/leaders-gainers-and-unexpected-winners-in-the-enterprise-ai-arms-race/)
- Companies are projected to delay 25% of planned 2026 AI spend by a year, mainly due to implementation friction with general-purpose tools (cited by CRV; original forecaster not confirmed) — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/vertical-ai-examples)
- Bessemer published a 2026 Healthcare AI ROI Scorecard (content not read) — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-2026-healthcare-ai-roi-scorecard)
- Vendor-side production evidence: Salesforce accounts with agents in production +70% q/q; ServiceNow AI ACV >$1B with target raised to $1.5B — [Salesforce FY27 Q2](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/08/26/fy27-q2-earnings/); [Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-news/servicenow-ai-crosses-1-bn-acv-as-enterprise-demand-lifts-q2-revenue)

### Inferences
- The MIT buy-vs-build finding (2:1 success for purchased tools) supports the commercial case for vertical/B2B AI vendors even while most internal pilots fail.
- Only ~22% of AI spend coming from software budgets (Menlo via SaaStr) implies the rest is funded from labour/services/new budget — consistent with outcome pricing in support and with the "experimental budget" risk when those budgets are reviewed.

### Gaps
- McKinsey State of AI 2026 (EBIT-impact figures) was not retrieved; no McKinsey data is included.
- The a16z 2026 survey's figure for innovation-budget share was not found (the 25% -> 7% figure is from the prior edition via a secondary source).
- The Menlo PDF and SaaStr summary could not be opened (network block on saastr.com; PDF not fetched), so pilot-to-production conversion rates and category detail are missing.
- No independent (non-vendor) ROI studies per category (e.g. measured cost per resolution, clinician time saved) were retrieved.
